# Character: Jeerdan Darcy
#cast/active #status/solid

> "He came through at the tail of the wet season, when we'd lost two men to the fever and the barley was going over. Didn't ask whose land it was. Just took the second plough and worked till dark, four days, and went on north. My lord's steward came through a fortnight later and took a third of it." — A smallholder of the river spur, Duchy of Aerthos

> "Show me the paper. That is all I have ever asked of him. Show me the paper, and I will call him warden and mean it." — [[Tythius De Vonce|Duke Tythius De Vonce]]

## 📊 Vital Statistics
- **Full Name / Aliases:** Jeerdan Darcy / "the Warden" / "Plough-Hand" (affectionate, in the hamlets; contemptuous, in the High Quarter)
- **Current Occupation:** Warden of the [[The Five Duchies of the Whispering Coast#🛣️ The Ducal Concord Road|Ducal Concord Road]] — arbitrates between the joint garrison detachments and keeps the Road open
- **Social Class / Standing:** Legally ambiguous. Claims **Landed by Commission**, the same mechanism that covers a [[The Golden Company|Golden Company]] officer — but no one has ever produced the instrument that commissioned him
- **Primary Residence:** None fixed. A warden's cot at each of the Road's four garrison posts, and whatever barn is nearest when the weather turns
- **Affiliations:** The Ducal Concord Road garrison (all four standing Duchies and the [[Council of Five|Council of Five]] jointly). No allegiance to any one of them, which is either the point of the office or its central defect, depending on who is asked

## ⚖️ Realism & Physicality
- **Age & Vitality:** 54. Nineteen years in the saddle on the same three hundred miles of road, and it shows in the hips and the lower back rather than anywhere dramatic. Still rides a full circuit; needs two days flat at the end of it now, where he used to need none.
- **Physical Flaws / Limitations:** Hands ruined twice over — sword calluses laid down young, then broken open and rebuilt every harvest since by scythe and plough-handle. The right hand no longer closes fully in cold weather, which is a genuine problem for a man whose authority has always rested partly on being visibly armed. A badly-set collarbone from a fall on the Stonereach leg sits high and crooked under the cloak.
- **Financial Status:** Poor, and paid in arrears. His stipend comes from the Road's joint upkeep purse, which means four ducal treasuries and the Council each assume one of the others has settled it. He has gone as long as fourteen months without seeing coin and has never once formally complained, because a formal complaint would require someone to look up which body owes him.
- **Equipment & Upkeep:** A good sword, twenty years old, maintained obsessively because it cannot be replaced on his income. A warden's grey cloak with no house badge on it — a deliberate blank where every other man on the Road wears a colour. A leather satchel of route-books, toll disputes, and letters he has been asked to carry between people who will not write to each other directly.

## 🧠 Psychology & Drive
- **Immediate Goal:** Get the Stonereach toll-keep and the De Vonce garrison detachment to stand down before the argument over winter provisioning becomes a killing, without either side asking him by what right he is intervening.
- **The Core Fear:** That someone finally forces the question. Not violence — the question. He does not know who appointed him. He was handed the cloak nineteen years ago by a predecessor who also could not name the appointing authority, and every attempt he has made to find the founding instrument has come back empty. If the **[[The Cult of the Zenith|Cult of the Zenith]]** is ever asked to check the Register for his commission and finds nothing, they will not merely decline to confirm him — under the Court of Nullity they will rule the office **void**, and they will be right.
- **Moral Compromises:** He has looked away, repeatedly. [[The Cobalt Feather Syndicate]] cargo moving north under bad manifests, a Divtown timber convoy that was not carrying only timber, an Aerthos steward's private levy collected outside any lawful assessment. Each time for the same reason: stopping it would mean invoking an authority he cannot prove he has, in front of witnesses, on a road where someone would eventually write it down.

## 📜 Backstory & Current Role
Jeerdan was born on the river spur in Aerthos, in one of the smallholdings the Road was later cut past — the son of tenants, not of a house. He took service as a garrison spearman young, transferred onto the Concord Road detachment in his twenties, and was still there at thirty-five when the previous warden handed him a grey cloak, a satchel of route-books, and no explanation.

Nineteen years later he is the only functioning institution between Port Nevarellon and the ducal seats. Where the coast has five capitals and effectively nothing recognised between them, Jeerdan is what "between them" has instead of a government: he settles garrison disputes that no chain of command can settle, arbitrates tolls, keeps the road-books, carries word between houses that will not correspond, and stops at every hamlet within a half-day of the Road to ask what has gone wrong since last season.

The ploughing is not a legend, though it has become one. When a village is short-handed at harvest — fever, levy, a bad birth season — he works. He has done it in all four duchies, which is precisely the problem: **a man who lends his back to an Aerthos smallholding in a bad year is, from the perspective of that smallholder's lord, interfering with the labour obligations that make the estate produce a surplus at all.** In Aerthos especially, where the grain trade is built on binding labour to the demesne, the peasantry's warden and the duchy's interest are not compatible, and Jeerdan has never pretended otherwise. He does not care which duke's ledger a village appears in. He has said so, in front of stewards, more than once.

To the smallholders he is the only authority that has ever arrived without taking something. To a Duke he is an unlicensed power operating inside sovereign territory, adored by the tenantry, wearing no colour and answering to no one — which is, historically, the exact description of a man a lord has hanged. That he has not been hanged is not restraint. It is that all four Duchies and the Council each suspect he might be one of the *others'* men, and none of them wants to be the one who moved first and was wrong.

He also, without ever having been told, sits on the coast's single most sensitive piece of ground. The Road and the city aqueduct share a corridor out of Port Nevarellon through the De Vonce foothills. The man who arbitrates the Road's garrison is the man standing over Port Nevarellon's water.
