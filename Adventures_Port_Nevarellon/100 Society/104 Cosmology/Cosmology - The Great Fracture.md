# Cosmology: The Great Fracture & The Infinite Disk
#lore/cosmology #lore/history #status/solid

> "We killed the anchors of the world to steal their sky. Now we drift on a broken plate, looking out into nothing, while the ghosts of our creators scratch at the edges of reality to get their bones back." — Ancient Elven Fragment, Year Unknown

> **Ground truth — GM-facing.** No one in Austhal knows this whole. What survives in the world is ruins, bones and fragments like the one quoted above. For what people actually believe, see [[Religion - The Pagan Pantheon and the Faith Domains]] and the faction notes.

## 🌌 The Primordial State: The Sphere
Before history began, existence was a perfect, closed, three-dimensional system—The Sphere. 
- There was no beginning, no end, and no decay. It was a flawless perpetual motion machine of reality.
- **The Creators:** Immense, nameless entities built this closed system. For them, the creation and maintenance of the Sphere was their ultimate purpose. 
- **The Experiment:** To test the limits of this infinite loop, the entities created the four mortal races—Humans, Elves, Dwarves, and Halflings—each entity designing a variant to see if any mortal could find a hidden beginning or end within the eternal cycle.

---

## 🩸 The Corruption of Individuation & The Naming
A catastrophic, unforeseen byproduct occurred when mortal life was introduced: **Individuation**.
As mortals began to experience distinct, separate lives, their consciousness fractured away from the unity of the universe. 

- **The Virus of Emotion:** This fragmentation birthed petty, volatile mortal emotions (greed, envy, ambition, fear). Because the creators were linked to their creations, this emotional rot bled upward.
- **The Naming:** The mortals began to name their creators. In this naming, they further infected the entities with **Identity**. Petty mortal Ego and Id shaped the nameless creators into the narrow visions of the mortal races. They became selfish, prideful, cruel, loving, and hateful—everything the mortals found in themselves. 
- **The Divide of Death:** For the first time, the entities fractured from the great oneness and became a pantheon of many. Anarchy bloomed. The Gods realized they were eternal within the Sphere, but the Mortals realized they were finite—each new mortal was unique, and when they died, they ceased to exist.

---

## 🔨 The Deicide & The Shattering
Driven by a desire for absolute freedom, immortality, and power, the mortals stole the magic and science of their creators. They weaponized this knowledge to execute a cosmic rebellion. The weight of this deicide tore the fabric of reality apart.

- **The Great Disk (The Material Plane):** The Sphere holding existence together collapsed. It was violently reshaped into a flat plane of physical matter so staggeringly vast that no known mortal has ever found its outer edge, nor dug deep enough to find the sky at the bottom.
- **The Void:** The surrounding infinity became an absolute nothingness in which the Disk drifts endlessly.
- **The Physical Remnants:** The Gods did not vanish; they were infinitely fractured. The Great Disk became the Material Plane, and scattered throughout its unfathomable depths are the physical, calcified remnants of the murdered Gods — and of the entities the Creators made for the war. The coast calls them **wrack** (see below).

## 🌊 The Tideways: The High Reach, The Slack Water, and The Undertow
When the mortals finally tore the Sphere apart, the scattered essence didn't drift at random. It settled the way disturbed water settles after a storm — into layers, currents, and stillness — shaped as much by the mortals' own guilt, hope, and terror as by whatever the entities had been before. Scholars and sailors call the whole layered structure **the Tideways**; what any one person calls a given layer usually says more about that person's temple, or lack of one, than about the layer itself.

```text
[ THE HIGH REACH ] --> Order, Law, Light, "Good"
          |
[ THE SLACK WATER ] --> The Great Disk (The Physical Anchor & The Wrack)
          |
[ THE UNDERTOW ] --> Chaos, Decay, Entropy, "Evil"
```

- **The High Reach:** The uppermost layer, where essence pulled by mortal hope and mortal longing for salvation drifted upward and outward. The pious call this **the Heavens**; sailors and scholars more often call it the Reach, since — like a tidal reach — it's a navigable extremity, not a throne room.
- **The Slack Water:** The calm, central layer — the Great Disk itself, the Material Plane, where the shattered wrack still lies half-buried in stone and silt. Mortals live here by default, in the still water between two opposing pulls, which is either the safest place in the Tideways or the most exposed, depending on who you ask. Most mortals just call it "the world" and never think of it as a layer at all — which is rather the point.
- **The Undertow:** The lowest layer, where essence twisted by mortal terror, guilt, and the raw violence of the pre-Sundering war sank and thickened. The pious call this **the Hells**; the Undertow is the more honest word, because it doesn't drag you down all at once — it just never lets go once it has you.

Because these are simply positions in a continuous, layered structure rather than separate absolute realities, a mortal — or anything else — can in principle be pulled from one layer to another, or reach into a layer that isn't theirs, provided they can survive the crossing. Very few do; most who try without a stabilizing anchor drown in it, one way or another.

**A note on the labels:** "Good" and "Evil" are mortal words laid over the High Reach and the Undertow after the fact, the same way mortal Ego and Identity were laid over the nameless entities during the Naming. The Reach and the Undertow are not inherently virtuous or wicked — they are simply where mortal collective feeling pulled the wreckage. A saint of the Reach can be a butcher who happened to die convinced he was righteous. A thing that crawled up out of the Undertow can be the only creature that ever told a slave the truth. Nothing in the Tideways is required to be good just because it floats, or evil just because it sinks.

## 🪨 Wrack
**Essence** is what the nameless entities were — and what the entities they made for the war were, some of them as vast as their makers. *God* is a mortal word, laid over them in the Naming; ground-truth notes say **essence**. In the Slack Water it takes many forms: the currents the cults call Domains, the fey that quickened in still water, the dead masses of the moons, and the rare intact remnant, like Tuwal Ghorun.

**Wrack** is its solid form: essence that settled into the Slack Water and hardened into stone, glass, bone and metal, the way a tide leaves its wrack along the shore. It lies everywhere, from trench-floors and marsh-beds to the war-fields and the deep works under the mountains.
- **A shard** is one piece of wrack.
- **Unanchored wrack** has never fully settled. It still bleeds essence. It is the valuable kind and the dangerous kind, and the Low Moons stir it.
- **On the coast** *black wrack* carries the Undertow and *pale wrack* carries the High Reach. These are Docks words. The GM-facing notes say *Undertow wrack* and *Reach wrack*.
- **Who handles it:** the Guild of Alchemists assays it under licence, the Cobalt Feather smuggles it, and a hag works it into flesh.

## 🌿 The Slack-Born: Fey and Hags
Not all the essence that settled in the Slack Water hardened into wrack. Where it pooled in still places — marsh, tarn, sheltered reef, the drowned edges of old forests — some of it quickened. **The fey** are what quickened: beings formed from essence that neither rose to the High Reach nor sank to the Undertow, and so belong wholly to the still water of the world. They are not a mortal race. The Creators never designed them, and they had no part in the War of Creation. Most mortals never meet one; those who do find them beautiful, unhurried, and unconcerned with mortal purposes.

**Hags** are humanoid fey — long-lived, rooted to a single place, and unlike the rest of their kind, willing to trade with mortals. Their stock in trade is change. A hag can work Undertow wrack into living flesh, and will, for a price that is always paid in full.

Where hags root, how they move, what they ask in payment and who hunts them: see [[Lore - Hags]]. What the land keeps of the people it kills: see [[Lore - Wretches]].
