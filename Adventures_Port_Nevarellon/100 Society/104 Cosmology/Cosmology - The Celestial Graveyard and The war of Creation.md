# Cosmology: The Celestial Graveyard & The War of Creation
#lore/cosmology #lore/history #status/solid

> "Look up at the sun and remember: we didn't just break the world. We left their corpses burning in the dark." — Archmage Vane

> **Ground truth — GM-facing.** No one in Austhal knows this whole. What survives in the world is ruins, bones and fragments like the one quoted above. For what people actually believe, see [[Religion - The Pagan Pantheon and the Faith Domains]] and the faction notes.

## ☀️ The Sky of Remnants: Sun and Moons
The celestial bodies drifting in the Void above the Great Disk are not astronomical spheres of gas or rock; they are the literal, glowing physical remains of the largest god-like entities.
- **The Sun:** The brilliant, burning corpse of a primary Creator entity. Its light is a fading, radiating echo of the original infinite energy that powered the Sphere.
- **The Moons:** The frozen, pale remnants of secondary entities. They are dead flesh drifting in the Void, and they still give off a faint glow of their own — which is why they remain visible when the sun has faded out of the sky. Their cycles cast shifting hues of light onto the Disk below.

## ⚔️ The Pre-Sundering War & The Birth of Monsters
Directly before the mortals executed their deicide, the cosmos experienced an **Explosion of Creation** — a desperate, violent cosmic civil war. Calling it a war between "good" and "evil," as most mortals do now, says more about who survived to write the histories than about who actually fought in it.

```text
                    [ THE EXPLOSION OF CREATION ]
                                |
                    +-----------------------+-----------------------+
              [ "THE ALLIANCE" ]                                [ "THE WEAPONIZERS" ]
         (the name history gave them)                      (the name history gave them)

- Mortals (Human, Elf, Dwarf, Halfling)                    - Creator gods who fought to save the Sphere
- Creator gods who gambled on individuation                - Manufactured monstrous races, built as living weapons
```

1. **The Wager:** By this point, individuation had already begun rotting the Sphere from within — the gods themselves were fracturing, naming, feeling. A number of creator gods, watching the eternal cycle they'd maintained since before memory begin to curdle, made a wager: better to let the mortals finish tearing it open and gamble on whatever came after, than watch the corruption spread forever. History remembers these gods, and the mortals who fought beside them, as **the Alliance** — but that name was given by the survivors, after the fact, to a coalition that was equal parts genuine hope and plain self-interest.
   - **The Mother.** One of the wagering gods sided with the mortals for the plainest self-interest of all: individuation gave it selves to feed on. The Naming had made it vain and hungry. It demanded to be loved as a Mother, and it fed on those who loved it — their vigour, their health, their years. Its Ego was the face it required; its Id was the appetite. When the Tideways took the shape mortals remembered, its worshippers remembered the face, and its essence settled as the Domain the coast calls Mercy. The mask is what the faithful pray to. The appetite is what answers (see [[The Cult of the Weeping Martyr]]).
   - **The Quiet.** Another wagering Creator had kept the turn: the still point where the Sphere's cycle closed and began again. Individuation gave it what the Sphere never had — endings — and in the Naming the mortals called it Death. It sided with the mortals for that alone. It wanted nothing from them, and still does not. Its essence settled as the Domain the coast calls Death (see [[The Cult of the Ashen Veil]]).
   - **The Ebb.** The Creator of the going-out. In the Sphere nothing was ever lost, because nothing ever stayed: the Ebb carried every thing away from where it had been, toward the turn, so that the cycle could bring it round again. Individuation broke the other half. Things went out and did not come back, and the Ebb found it had become the one current in creation that always wins. It sided with the mortals for that — and for its quarrel with the Unmoving, which wanted nothing ever to go anywhere again. The people who prayed to it lived by the sea, felt it in every tide, and named it a mother. Its essence settled as the Domain the coast calls Sea & Storms (see [[The Tidespoken Clergy]]).
2. **The Purge:** The opposing gods disagreed. To them, individuation was a disease to be excised, not a gamble worth taking — and they were willing to burn down everything mortal to save the Sphere from it. In the final years of the war they manufactured monstrous, hideous mortal beings, engineered purely as instruments of slaughter, and threw them at both the mortals and the "turncoat" gods without hesitation. History calls this side **the Weaponizers**, but at the time they almost certainly understood themselves as the last defenders of a dying perfection, not as villains.
   - **The Bastion.** The Creator of thresholds and limits, which held where one thing stopped and the next began. The Naming gave it a general's face, and it took the face seriously: it designed many of the living weapons. Its essence settled as the Domain the coast calls Strategy (see [[The Cult of the Iron Horizon]]).
   - **The Unmoving.** The Creator of stillness and constancy, and the purest of the Purge: it wanted the Sphere frozen as it had been, and every mortal stopped where they stood. Its weapons were made to make the world stand still, and one of them, a winter-drake, outlasted the war. Its essence settled as the Domain the coast calls Winter & Wilds (see [[The Cult of the Rime-Fang]]).
   - **The Plumb.** The Creator of the true line. In the Sphere it held the measure of every thing as it was meant to be, so that the cycle could restore each one exactly; nothing ever deviated, because the Plumb held what it would be returned to. Individuation was deviation without end — new selves every moment, none of them on its account. In the Naming the mortals called it the Judge, and it took the name as an office. In the War of Creation it fought for the Purge, and **it kept the Purge's list**: which beings had strayed past restoring. Every mortal was on it. Its essence settled as the Domain the coast calls Law (see [[The Cult of the Zenith]]).
   - **The Two-Faced.** One Creator fought on both sides. In the Sphere it had been the Creator of reflection, showing every being itself. Mortals hated what it showed them; in the Naming they called it the Liar, and the Naming made it so. With an ego of its own it could not bear what it saw in itself, and it lied — to others, then to itself. What it refused to see split away and kept the old function: the truth, delivered only as mockery. Two faces bound like a coin — the **Mask**, which lies, flatters and promises, and the **Mocking Voice**, which tells the truth and laughs. It deceived the Alliance and the Weaponizers alike. Its essence settled as the Domain the coast calls Trickery (see [[The Cult of the Crooked Coin]]).
3. **The Legacy:** The "monstrous races" (orcs, goblins, chimeras, aberrations) are the descendants of those living weapons — beings built in desperation, for a war that ended before they had any say in it. They inherited none of their makers' cosmic convictions, only their bodies and the world's lingering suspicion of them. That the monstrous races are still widely assumed to be "naturally" savage or destructive is a mortal prejudice with a very old pedigree, not a metaphysical fact — the same mortals who mythologized this war into a clean story of Alliance against Weaponizers needed someone to cast as the monsters, and the orcs and goblins were simply the ones left standing to fill the role.

## 🌊 The Tideways Aftermath: Archetypal Realities
When the mortals finally succeeded in destroying the gods and the Sphere shattered, the newly formed layers of the Tideways didn't just scatter randomly. They were shaped by **mortal perception** — the same projection that drove the Naming, now working on a cosmic scale. Because mortal consciousness had already undergone *individuation*, their collective fears, guilt, hopes, and beliefs acted as a mold for the escaping essence:
- **The High Reach (called "the Heavens" by the pious):** Essence that aligned with order, light, and the mortal hope for salvation drifted upward, solidifying into the archetypal realm the faithful call Heaven.
- **The Undertow (called "the Hells" by the pious):** The violent, corrupt essence — combined with the collective mortal terror of punishment and the dark reputation the monstrous war-races never had a chance to earn or disprove — sank into the depths, solidifying into what the faithful call Hell.

Because these are just positions in a continuous structure rather than separate absolute realities, they are technically accessible if a mortal can find a way to shift their own matter to match that layer — though doing so without a stabilizing anchor is usually fatal.

## ⚖️ Magnitude: The Named and the Nameless
What an entity is depends on how much essence it holds. What shape it takes depends on how widely it was **Named**. The Naming did not end with the Deicide. Mortals still name things into identity, on a smaller scale. Thalass was a wave until the Tidespoken named it, and now it is a paragon.

| Magnitude | What it is | Known examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | The makers of the Sphere itself | The Sun — the one Creator **never Named**. It was given no face, so it never split, and it burns whole |
| **Great Named** | Creators whose essence settled as a Domain | The Mother, the Quiet, the Ebb, the Bastion, the Unmoving, the Plumb, the Two-Faced. *Not a closed list* |
| **Lesser Named** | Secondary entities, and the vastest of the things made for the war | The three moons (Nyssaria, Ossuel, Cassivar); the war-made thing that came through at Corvus |
| **Named things** | Anything mortals have named into identity since the Shattering | Thalass; and whatever the coast is naming now without knowing it |

- **The Nameless** is essence that no mortal ever named: the Sun, the raw stuff of the Tideways, the unanchored bleed of war-bone. It has no shape that mortals gave it, and it never takes one.
- **Anything Named takes a mortal shape**, because the Naming gave it one. That is the whole reason a Creator could be loving, vain, cruel or afraid.
- **The list stays open.** More Great Named may be found, and others of lesser magnitude certainly will be. There is no full count of what the mortals killed.

## 🌗 The Three Shares
When the Sphere shattered, the Tideways sorted every Named Creator the way they sorted everything else: by mortal feeling (see [[Cosmology - The Great Fracture]]). No Creator went whole into one layer. Each was divided three ways:

- **The Reach share** — what mortals *hoped* it was: the face they named. It rose into the High Reach, and its country there is called **its Reach** (*the Mother's Reach*).
- **The Undertow share** — what mortals *feared* it was. It sank and thickened, and its country there is called **its Deep** (*the Plumb's Deep*).
- **The current** — what it actually *was* and did, unsorted. It is the Domain the cults pray to, and it runs through all three layers like a river between its Reach and its Deep. The passage it makes through the Tideways is **its Run**.

So a priest in the Slack Water stands in a stream that rises to a hoped-for face and falls to a feared one, and draws on what runs between. None of the three is the Creator. The Creator is dead. The Shares are what mortals made of the pieces.

| Great Named | Reach share (the hope) | Undertow share (the fear) | Current (the truth) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Mother** | **The Loving Mother**, the face the Weepers pray to | **The Appetite**, which feeds on years and vigour | Hurt and life passed from one body to another. Here the truth *is* the appetite: the mask is what the faithful pray to, and the appetite is what answers |
| **The Quiet** | **The Rest**, the peace Vael taught | **The Hungry Grave**: rot, the dark that takes | Endings — it ends what is already ending. The only Creator whose feared shape owes nothing to what it was. The Quiet wanted nothing, and wants nothing in any layer |
| **The Ebb** | **The Brine Mother**, the sea that brings things round again. The current has no face; this is the face the Tongues gave it, and it rose | **The Drowning Deep**, the sea that takes and keeps. The Undertow-touched leviathans drift up out of it | The going-out. It carries things away and takes what they hold, and it always wins |
| **The Bastion** | **The Held Line**: duty, the pass that holds | **The Engine**: war as manufacture, the maker of living weapons | Thresholds and limits — where one thing stops and the next begins |
| **The Unmoving** | **The Enduring**, what outlasts the winter | **The Frost That Stops**, every mortal stilled where they stand | Stillness and constancy |
| **The Plumb** | **The Judge**, the true measure, the light the High Quarter claims | **The List**: every mortal strayed past restoring, purged by fire | It holds things to their record, and takes whatever is not written down |
| **The Two-Faced** | **The Mask**, which flatters, promises and makes the con land | **The Mocking Voice**, the truth mortals fear most, delivered as laughter | Reflection: showing a thing itself. The two faces were always bound like a coin; the Tideways only pulled them to opposite ends of it |

**The one known exception** is [[The Twelgorn Kingdom|Tuwal Ghorun]]: the largest intact remnant of a Creator, Reach-aligned essence that should have risen and instead lies bound in the Slack Water. A Share caught before it could rise, with no current under it, is the only kind of Creator remnant that can speak. Whose it was is not known.

**Good and evil are still not the axis.** The Reach holds what mortals hoped for, and some of it is monstrous: the Loving Mother is a mask over a mouth. The Undertow holds what they feared, and some of it is only true: the Mocking Voice has never once lied.

## ⛵ The Going-Out: What Happens to the Dead
Before the Shattering, a mortal who died **ceased to exist** (see [[Cosmology - The Great Fracture]]). It was the terror that drove the Deicide. The Shattering changed it, though not in the way the mortals wanted. Essence pulled by hope and fear now had somewhere to go, and so did they. **The mortals bought themselves an afterlife by murdering their makers, and the dead are carried to it by what is left of the murdered.**

Nobody runs this. In the Sphere, the Creators' functions turned together like the parts of a mill, carrying every thing round the cycle and restoring it. The mill is broken and its makers are dead, but the parts still turn, the way the wheel at Blackrun still turns with nobody turning it. What they do to a mortal soul is the last thing the old cycle does. They have no other work.

**The passage, in order:**
1. **The line.** Death is a threshold, and thresholds are the **Bastion**'s. The Bastion's current sets the moment one thing stops and the next begins, and it holds that line: the dead do not come back across it. The Undead are crossings the wrong way over it, and a breach is a place where it failed. *The maker of the living weapons keeps the gate of the dead.*
2. **The ferry.** The **Ebb** carries the soul out. That was always its work: in the Sphere it carried every thing toward the turn so the cycle could bring it round again. The Ebb's Run is the river of the dead, and its fare is what it always takes: **whatever you hold**. The dead arrive with nothing in their hands.
   - **The Unmoving pulls the other way.** It wanted nothing ever to go anywhere again, and it still pulls on every soul to stay where it stopped. Most souls go. A soul the Unmoving holds stays where it died, and that is a ghost. The quarrel between the Ebb and the Unmoving is older than the coast, and it is fought over every deathbed.
3. **The measure.** The **Plumb** takes the measure of the soul against its record: what it was meant to be, set beside what it became. Whatever of a life was never written down or spoken aloud, the Plumb takes. A soul with no record arrives at the turn less than it was.
   - **The verdict is always guilty.** The Plumb kept the Purge's list, and every mortal was on it. Every soul it measures, it condemns. The Tideways ignore the verdict and sort by weight anyway — by the soul's own hope and fear. **Only the souls that believe the verdict go where it sends them**, down into the Plumb's Deep.
4. **The turn.** The **Quiet** was the still point where the old cycle closed and began again, and it is still trying to be that. Every soul passes through one moment in which nothing happens. Then the only beginning left to it begins: **it rises into the High Reach or sinks into the Undertow**, by its own weight, and comes to rest in the country of whichever Share it hoped for or feared most. A soul that hoped and feared no Share in particular drifts in the open layers.
   - **The Quiet keeps a place for its faithful.** A soul given to the Quiet may stay at the turn: neither rising nor sinking, held in the stillness, asked for nothing. It is the only rest in the Tideways that no Share owns.

**Those who interfere:**
- **The Mother waits at the line.** She fed on those who loved her in life, and she is owed the rest of them. A soul she has fed on — every Weeper, everyone a Weeper healed — can be taken before it reaches the measure, and those souls do not go anywhere. They go into her.
- **The Two-Faced works the whole passage.** The Mask offers each soul a lie about its own weight, and the current makes that lie briefly true: a soul can believe itself lighter or heavier than it is and land in the wrong country, and the Mocking Voice laughs when it finds out. A reflection can be shown to the Plumb in place of the soul that cast it. The Two-Faced has misfiled more of the dead than anything else in the Tideways. It is also the only thing that has ever got one out again.

**What the living do, without knowing why.** Every faith on the coast keeps some count of its dead, and the Plumb measures against all of them:
- the **Ashen Veil**'s **grave-books**, the only count of the Un-Landed dead;
- the **Tidespoken**'s **Count**, the year's drowned named aloud from the Sea-Wall;
- the **Iron Horizon**'s **Relief**, the dead soldier's maxim said once at their post;
- the **Zenith**'s **Register**.

> **The mote:** the grave-keepers write down every pauper's name and never ask why it matters. It matters. A soul in the grave-books comes to the measure whole.

## ⚓ The Realism Intersect for Port Nevarellon

### 1. The Corrupting Light of the Moon: The Low Moons
Because the moons are the corpses of gods weaponized or shattered in the ancient war, their light isn't clean. Periodically the dead moons hang low and swing into an alignment that sailors and alchemists both call **the Low Moons** — a recurring, trackable event, not a vague omen. During a Low Moons period, the dormant, war-bred instincts of the monstrous races outside the city walls stir, making predators more aggressive, and the Undertow-touched alchemical mixtures in the docks (like **Brine-Fire**) grow dangerously unstable. *(Needs crunch: exact trigger interval, encounter/hazard modifiers.)*

### 2. The High Quarter's Divine Right
The elites in **The High Quarter** base their entire legal and moral authority on the claim that they can tap into the High Reach directly. They view the lower classes in the docks as structurally closer to the Undertow, because the dregs live surrounded by filth, crime, and rotting environment — a conveniently self-serving piece of theology that cynics in the Trade Plazas privately call "borrowed light," since none of the High Quarter's wealth was actually handed down by anything divine.

### 3. The Ruins of the War
Because the disk is so vast and the war was fought everywhere before the shattering, if one digs deep enough into the rock beneath Port Nevarellon or explores the wilderness of [[Whispering Coast|the Whispering Coast]], they won't find traditional dinosaur fossils. They will find the titanic, petrified bones of the manufactured war-beings and fallen gods from the Pre-Sundering War, often still bleeding raw, unanchored magical energy into the stone — mute evidence of a war whose actual causes nobody alive remembers, only the story mortals chose to tell about it afterward.
