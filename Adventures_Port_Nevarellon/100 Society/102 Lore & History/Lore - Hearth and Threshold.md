# Lore: Hearth and Threshold
#lore/society #lore/creatures #status/draft

> "Don't ask me why. My mother didn't know either. Do it anyway, and teach it to yours." — a homestead wife, west of the Barley Flats

> **GM reference.** The body of each custom is in-world: what people do, where, and what they say it is for. The verdict under it is ground truth: **works**, **half**, **useless** or **harmful**. Nobody on the coast knows which is which. They keep all of them, the way they keep every other habit they were raised in, and they will defend the useless ones as hard as the rest.

---

## 🏠 What this note is
The things ordinary people do every day because of what lives in the dark, kept by people who could not tell you where any of it came from.

- **On the coast** the customs are habit. There is a grave-keeper in every parish, a Copper on the corner and a Tongue on the Sea-Wall, and most people keep the customs the way they knock on wood.
- **In the wild inland** the customs are all there is. West of the duchies' last ploughland there is no duke, no Veil and no Company for weeks in any direction (see [[Whispering Coast]]). A homestead that forgets one does not get a second warning. The inland keeps the same customs harder, and keeps a few the coast has forgotten.

The coast's customs are, by and large, older and more right than the coast's priests. They are also wrong in some places that matter.

## 🚪 At the Door

### Turn the glass
At dusk, a mirror is turned to face the wall. Where there is no mirror, the washbasin is tipped out and the water-butt lidded. Nobody looks into standing water after dark, and nobody lets a child do it. On the coast it is a rich house's habit, because glass is a rich house's possession. Inland, it is the basin and the well-cover, and it is kept in every house. *"Don't give it a door,"* people say, and do not say what *it* is.
> **GM-facing — works.** The Mocking Voice can only come to someone through a reflection: a mirror, still water, a polished blade. Turn the glass and it has nowhere to stand. The **glass-readers** keep their mirrors uncovered, because their whole craft is a reflection made to act (see [[Lore - Arcana]]). The neighbours notice, and some of them move house.

### The door-sitter
When someone in the house is sick, someone else sits at the door all night. Nobody comes in who was not let in by the household — not a neighbour, not a midwife, not a priest who was not sent for. Inland, the sitter keeps a lamp lit and a cudgel by the stool.
> **GM-facing — half.** It does nothing against the Loving Mother, who comes to a sickbed without using the door (*"Nobody let her in"* — see [[Lore - Likenesses and Dreads]]). It does stop **the Nurse**: a War-Made carrier of the Mother's, in a midwife's shape, which holds whatever it finds and keeps it alive while it takes the years out of it (see [[Lore - The War-Made]]). The Nurse has a body. It has to come in through something.

### Iron over the lintel
A cold-iron nail is driven over the door. An iron horseshoe goes in the cradle, and marsh-guides wear an iron nail on a cord. The coast believes iron keeps out everything that walks at night.
> **GM-facing — works, narrowly.** Cold iron answers the fey and nothing else (see [[Lore - Hags]]). Against a hag's work or a Pool-Child it is the best thing a poor household owns. Against a Wretch, a Dread or a War-Made thing it is a nail.

### The asking
Inland, someone who comes home after a long time away is not let in at once. The household asks them something only the two of them could know. Older households also take the returner's hand at the door, turn it to the lamp, and check the scars are where they left them.
> **GM-facing — half.** The question is useless. **The Come-Home** — a double, the Two-Faced's War-Made, worn so long it believes it is the person — carries its original's memories and will answer correctly (see [[Lore - The War-Made]]). The hand is not useless. A double was made as a perfect *reflection* of one soldier, and a reflection is reversed: its scars sit on the wrong side, its parting falls the wrong way, and it favours the other hand. Households that still check the hand catch it. Households that only ask the question let it sit down to supper.

### If the bob hangs, go
Masons hang a plumb-line in the doorway of every new house to show it is built true, and leave the bob hanging until the roof is on. Inland there is an older saying: **if you wake and find a small iron weight hanging over your bed from nothing, you leave that night**, and you do not take the bed.
> **GM-facing — works.** The masons' line is craft pride and does nothing. The saying is exact. A **Plumb-bob** is the least of the Plumb's instruments: a fist-sized drop of wrack that drifts loose from the ruins where the measurers stand and hangs over whatever has strayed from its line — the hag-worked, the Undertow-touched, a body that has carried black wrack, and anyone who works a spell (see [[Lore - The War-Made]]). It marks the place for a measurer. The measurer comes to the mark. By then, the people who know the saying are gone.

## 💧 Water

### Wake the water
Inland, a stock-tank is stirred with a pole at dusk, and nobody drinks at a waterhole alone without first throwing a stone in *"to wake it."* Children are taught to drink in pairs. On the coast the same habit survives as a rhyme about rain-barrels.
> **GM-facing — half.** Moving water does not quicken; that much is true, and the Tidespoken have built a faith on it (see [[The Tidespoken Clergy]]). Stirring a tank every evening stops anything new forming in it. It does nothing about what already has. A **Pool-Child** is a small fey quickened in a forgotten tank, a dam or a sheltered waterhole: child-sized, cold, and old enough to be patient. It is not a hag and does not bargain. It pulls in whatever drinks alone. The inland is mostly still water, and so it is mostly fey ground.

### Don't answer from the water
Never answer a voice that calls from under the harbour steps, from below a jetty or from the far side of a ford. Never wave back to a swimmer at night. Dockers' children are taught it before they can swim.
> **GM-facing — works.** The Drowning Deep beckons from just under the surface, and answering is the first step down (see [[Lore - Likenesses and Dreads]]). A Dread wants only what it wants. Refuse it the answer and it has nothing to hold.

### Knock the hull
Before a ship crosses deep water, a sailor raps the hull three times below the waterline, *"to tell what's under that we're not cargo."* Every fishing crew does it. The Oakhaven fleets do it twice.
> **GM-facing — harmful.** Three knocks on a hull was the Alliance's loading signal, from the age when the leviathans carried whole peoples out of the Purge's reach (see [[Lore - The War-Made]]). The leviathans soured in the Drowning Deep, but they still carry, and three knocks still tells them a hold is ready. The custom has outlived the knowledge and kept the signal word for word. It is how a careful crew calls one up.

### The fare in the fist
Before the drowned are given to the ebb, someone presses a coin into the hand and closes the fingers over it. The Tidespoken do not teach it and do not stop it.
> **GM-facing — works.** The Brine Mother's ferry takes as its fare whatever the dead are holding (see [[Cosmology - The Tideways]]). A dead hand closed on nothing pays with something else — a memory, a face, the name of a child. A coin is the cheapest fare there is, and the poorest families on the coast find one.

## 🕯️ Sickroom and Deathbed

### The Grey Lady's stair
On the stair of the House of Mercy, families leave a cup of honey-water and ask **the Grey Lady** to come to a sick child. Those who sit on the stair see a woman in a grey cloak go up. The child, afterwards, says its mother sat with it all night. The child gets better. The Weepers do not discourage it, because what the families describe looks like their own miracle (see [[The Cult of the Weeping Martyr]]).
> **GM-facing — harmful.** The Grey Lady is a Likeness of the **Loving Mother**, and the stair is the one place the coast has learned to call her. She wears the face the patient loves best and weeps gold, and the child recovers. Ten years on, the child is greyer than they should be, and slower to want anything (see [[Lore - Likenesses and Dreads]]). The Weepers' golden tears are the same Share's trade, which is why it looks like their miracle: it is.

### Open the window for the quiet
When someone is dying, a window is opened, however cold the night, so the quiet can come in. In the morning there are sometimes feathers on the sill.
> **GM-facing — useless.** The Rest does not need a window, and comes the same with every shutter barred. Nobody has ever been harmed by the custom, though, and the feathers on the sill make every family that sees them believe in it. It is a kindness to the living, and the coast is short of those.

## ⚰️ The Dead

### Hedge Featherfall
Inland, there is no grave-keeper for weeks, so the family lays their own dead down. They use hen feathers, or gull feathers if they have them, and say no words over the grave. Afterwards they carve the name into the post and say it aloud once. The Ashen Veil, when they hear of it, do not object.
> **GM-facing — works.** What keeps a body in the ground is that someone kept it, not who. A Featherfall done by a farmer's wife with hen feathers counts the same as one done by a keeper with gull feathers bought by the sack from Gullstand (see [[The Cult of the Ashen Veil]]). This is the inland's quiet mercy: the Veil's rite was always simple enough to be done by anyone who loved the dead.

### Stones on the chest
A stockman who dies out in the open is buried where he fell, with flat stones laid across his chest *"so he can't sit up."*
> **GM-facing — useless, edging into harmful.** A Wretch is the place holding on, not the body getting up, and the stones are no help (see [[Lore - Wretches]]). The harm is the first half of the custom. Burying a man on the very ground that killed him gives the place exactly what it wanted to keep. The Veil would carry him out, to fire or dry earth somewhere else.

### Say them in
Inland travellers carry a **name-cord**, knotted once for every homestead that has taken them in. At each door they give their name and where they are from, and the household says both back to them before anyone eats. A traveller who arrives with a long cord is trusted. One who arrives with none is asked why.
> **GM-facing — works.** A mortal's anchor in the Tideways is their name, held by others: said, written, remembered (see [[Cosmology - The Tideways]]). Every homestead that says a traveller's name back is a rope holding them. It is the strongest custom on the coast and the cheapest, and the inland does it for strangers.

### A mark, not a name
The Un-Landed of Port Nevarellon sign the ledgers of landlords, bond-houses and moneylenders with a scratched mark, never their name, even those who can write it. *"Don't give them your name,"* their mothers said. Most think it is about the law.
> **GM-facing — a trade-off.** The List reads only names that are written down, and every name it reads is found wanting. A mark is never read out. But a name that is never written is held more weakly, and the poor of the city are anchored by almost nothing. The only place most of them are ever written is the Veil's grave-book: their name goes down once, when it can no longer hurt them and can still hold them.

## 🔥 Fire, Work and Cold

### Bank the forge
Every forge on the coast goes cold one night in every turn. Smiths let the fire die, sweep out the ash, and do not light it again until morning. Nobody finishes a blade alone after dark.
> **GM-facing — works.** The Engine gathers at a forge that is never out (see [[Lore - Likenesses and Dreads]]). One cold night a turn is enough to keep it from settling. **Hollowforge never banks**: the smelting works run through every night of the year, and that is why the Engine is seen there (see [[Duchy of De Vonce]]). The masters of Hollowforge know the custom. They have done the arithmetic on a night's lost iron.

### Keep moving in the frost
In a hard frost, nobody sits down. People stamp, keep talking and walk in circles if there is nowhere to walk to. Inland drovers say it plainly: *"A man who sits down in the snow has said yes."*
> **GM-facing — works.** The Frost That Stops wants everything to stand still where it is, and it gathers around anyone freezing alone (see [[Lore - Likenesses and Dreads]]). Moving is refusing it. The Sleet Vigil's light in the window is the same answer, kept by the people indoors (see [[The Cult of the Rime-Fang]]).

### The empty place
Watch rosters on the coast leave one place on the wall unfilled, and the sergeant does not fill it. Inland, drovers sleeping out leave a bedroll's width of space by the fire.
> **GM-facing — works.** The Held Line takes the post beside you, and it needs the post to be there (see [[Lore - Likenesses and Dreads]]). On the worst night, the empty place is filled. The Iron Horizon is right that whoever fills it looks like Senecus (see [[The Cult of the Iron Horizon]]).

## 🎲 Table and Oath

### Never on one throw
No gambler worth the name stakes everything on a single throw. Stakes are split, the last coin is kept back, and a player who goes all in is pulled away from the table by their friends.
> **GM-facing — harmful.** The Mask, the smiling stranger who never loses, wants someone to believe their own coin, and it leaves only when someone stakes everything on one throw and wins it (see [[Lore - Likenesses and Dreads]]). A den full of careful players never gives it that, and the Mask stays at the table all night, every night. The dens that keep the custom best are the ones the stranger never leaves.

### Swear on the Line
A bargain on the coast is sealed with *"on the Line"* — a clasp of hands, or a thumb run down a doorframe. It is said a hundred times a day, in every market, and usually means nothing more than *"I mean it."*
> **GM-facing — half.** Usually nothing happens. But the Plumb's current holds things to their record (see [[The Cult of the Zenith]]), and an oath sworn on its name in front of a large enough crowd can gather something to hear it. If the oath was true, the **Judge** comes to the gallery. If it was false, the **List** comes, and reads the name.
