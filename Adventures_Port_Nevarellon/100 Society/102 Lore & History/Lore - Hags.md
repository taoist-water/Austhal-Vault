# Lore: Hags
#lore/creatures #lore/cosmology #status/draft

> "Hags always agree. That's the whole trouble with them." — Morgran "Fin" Deep-Draught, to a client who asked

> **GM reference.** Ground truth, except where marked as what people say. What the coast actually knows is in the sayings, the marsh-folk's habits and the hunters' reasons.

---

## 🌿 What a hag is
A hag is one of the **Slack-Born** — essence that pooled in a still place and quickened, rather than rising to the High Reach or sinking to the Undertow (see [[Cosmology - The Great Fracture]]). She is humanoid where most fey are not, long-lived past any mortal reckoning, and **rooted to a still place**. For most of her long life a hag does not travel. She is found, which is the first price of dealing with her.

She is also the only fey that trades. The rest of the Slack-Born are beautiful, unhurried and indifferent to mortal purposes. A hag is interested, which is worse.

## 🌊 Where they root
Every hag's ground is **still water, or a place where still water used to be**. The coast names four kinds, by the water that made them:

- **Bog-Hags** — the fen-mothers of the [[Silted Marshes]], down the Grey Veins where the channels stop moving. Leeches in the hair, peat under the nails. The marsh-folk know roughly where each one is and steer their skiffs wide.
- **Brine-Hags** — in sheltered salt water: lagoons, tide-pools, the calm reefs behind the Shield Atolls of [[The Inner Sea]]. Never the open ocean; nothing quickens in water that moves. Sailors' stories make them prettier than they are.
- **Briar-Hags** — in drowned woodland, where old forest has gone under at a marsh's edge and the trees stand dead in still water. Barefoot at the treeline, holding a lantern the wrong colour, and lovely.
- **Mirage-Hags** — in the places the water left: dried tarns, dead oases, salt pans. Beautiful, offering a cup at a well that is not there. She is always thirsty, and she takes it out on whoever comes.

## 🌒 Uprooting: the Low Moons
A hag is rooted, but not forever. **During the Low Moons** — the nine to twelve nights, every 840 days, when all three moon-corpses hang low and wrong (see [[Framework - The Coastal Reckoning]]) — the grip of the place she quickened in slackens, and **a hag can pull up her roots and move**.

She cannot go just anywhere. She needs **her own kind of ground** to put her roots back down in: a Bog-Hag another stagnant fen, a Brine-Hag another sheltered reef or lagoon, a Briar-Hag another drowned wood, a Mirage-Hag another place where still water used to be. She must reach it before the moons rise again, or she is caught between, and nobody knows what happens to a hag caught between because nobody has found one.

This is how a hag reaches a place no hag has been before, and why the marsh-folk count the Low Moons tables as closely as the Guild does. When the moons come down, the fen-mother at the end of the channel may not be where she was — and something may have arrived at the dry tarn up the road that was empty last cycle. The next Opening-season Low Moons (~59 A.A.) is close enough that the old guides have started saying so.

## 🎭 The face she wears
The fey are beautiful. Hags were too, once. A hag's trade is working Undertow wrack into living flesh, and over centuries she has done it to herself as much as to anyone — the Undertow's pull shows in her the way it shows in the people she changes. The Bog-Hags and Brine-Hags wear it openly. The Briar-Hags and Mirage-Hags wear a **glamour** over it, and the glamour holds until it doesn't: a blade of cold iron, a real wound, or the moment she stops bothering. Marsh-folk say the ugly ones are the honest ones. Marsh-folk have not met many of either.

## ⚖️ The trade
A hag's stock is **change**, and a hag **always agrees**. Bring her a shard of wrack and tell her what you want to become, and she will make you into it — gills, bark for skin, eyes that drink the dark. [[Morgran the Abomination|Morgran]] is the coast's best-known customer.

What she will not do is supply the shard. That has to be brought, which is why the [[The Cobalt Feather Syndicate|Cobalt Feather]]'s wrack trade has buyers in places with no alchemists in them.

**The price is always paid in full.** What she asks for is rarely coin:
- **Years** off the far end of a life.
- **A memory** — one specific face, song or road home, gone for good.
- **A sense** — taste, smell, colour, or a reflection.
- **A service**, named on the spot and done before the season turns.
- **A name.** People who knew you remember someone. Not you.
- **Something not yet yours** — the next thing you love, the next fortune, the first child.

A hag keeps the **letter** of what she agrees to, exactly, and expects the same. Refuse the price, or leave the service undone, and the work stays where she put it — she simply takes something else instead, and does not haggle a second time.

Anyone she has worked on is a little fey afterwards. Cold iron bites them now, and so does anything else that hunts the fey.

## 🕸️ Covens
A lone hag can change a body. A **coven** can do more. Hags form covens **to increase their power and their influence over change** — pooling what each has quickened from until the coven can work changes no single hag could manage, hold sway over a wider stretch of still water, and put its mark on more of the people who come to trade.

A coven forms where hags share ground, and the Low Moons are when it grows: a hag who uproots will often go to where a coven already stands rather than to empty water. The eldest is the coven's **mother**, and the others are hers the way her teeth are hers. A coven-mother can pass a death-wound onto a sister standing near her, and the coven bleeds so that she does not. Cold iron cuts that thread.

A coven is the difference between a story the marsh-folk tell and a problem a city has to answer.

## 🗝️ Bargaining for her life
A hag beaten to her knees does not beg; she **names her price**. A true answer, a curse lifted, safe passage through her ground. She keeps the letter of it — and whoever accepts is bound by its letter in turn.

## 🔥 Who hunts them
- **[[The Tidespoken Clergy]] hunt hags.** To the Tidespoken, the ocean is the **Brine Mother**, the true sovereign of the world, and her teaching is the sea's: everything moves, everything is taken by the tide in its turn. A hag is an **abomination** to that teaching twice over — a thing quickened in water that refused to move, which then takes the Undertow into its hands and works it into living flesh. The clergy hold that anything shaped from Undertow wrack belongs to the Undertow, which is why they want [[Morgran the Abomination|Morgran]] burned; they want the hag who made him burned first.
  The hags' best protection has always been where they root — marsh and reef, where the Tidespoken's reach is thinnest. The Low Moons are the one time a hag is out of her ground and on the move, and the Tidespoken know it.
- **Marsh-guides** carry a cold-iron nail on a cord and do not explain why.

> **The Mote:** Every hag has to be asked. Not one has ever come into a village and taken something unbidden; the price is terrible, but it is always a price, and it is always the customer's to refuse.
