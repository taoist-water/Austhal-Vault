# Lore: Wretches
#lore/creatures #status/draft

> "The Silt doesn't kill you with a blade." — Morgran the Abomination. *What it does afterwards, he doesn't talk about.*

> **GM reference.** Ground truth, except where marked as what people say. What the coast actually knows is in the sayings, the marsh-folk's habits and the hunters' reasons.

---

## 💀 What a Wretch is
A Wretch is **what a place keeps** after it has killed someone. Not a curse laid on a corpse by anyone in particular — the place itself, refusing to let go. The Undertow, the sailors say, doesn't drag you down all at once; it just never lets go once it has you (see [[Cosmology - The Great Fracture]]). Where Undertow wrack lies buried in the deep mud, the cold or the ash, that is literal.

A Wretch does not remember being a person. It remembers **being cold, or drowning, or thirsty**, and it walks toward whatever is warm and alive to make the feeling stop.

## 🗺️ The three the coast knows
- **Bog-Wretches** — the drowned of the [[Silted Marshes]] and of anywhere else freezing water closes over someone: flooded sluices, tidal mud. Waterlogged, slow, and always in threes and fours.
- **Grave-Wretches** — the frozen dead of the high passes and the barrows, where the winds come down off [[The Jagged Spine]] in the Winter Moons. Frost-cracked; worst in a blizzard.
- **Dust-Wretches** — the dead of thirst, heat and ash. **They walk the Corvus Scar**, and they are the commonest Wretches on the coast. When Corvus Spire fell in 8 A.A. and the Ash-Blight came down over the valley, it choked everyone it caught, and the Scar kept them (see [[The Five Duchies of the Whispering Coast]]). Sun-split and soot-black, brittle, and they go up like kindling — the one mercy of meeting them. The Scar-Holders who live in its clean pockets know the Dust-Wretches the way marsh-folk know the tide: when they come, how far, and that you do not argue with them.

Each is untroubled by the very thing that killed it. A Bog-Wretch does not mind the water; a Grave-Wretch does not mind the snow; a Dust-Wretch breathes the Blight without coughing. The living who follow it in do.

## 🌫️ Wretches and the Lost
The **Lost** of the Silted Marshes — travellers and escaped slaves driven feral by the fever and the wrack in the mud — are living. A Wretch is what the marsh keeps afterwards. Marsh-folk will tell you that a Lost who dies in the Silt does not always stay down, and that this is the whole reason they burn their own dead on the stilt-platforms rather than sinking them.

## 🌿 Wretches and hags
Hags do not make Wretches. A Wretch is nobody's work and nobody's purchase; there was no price and no bargain, only a place and a death. But they share ground. A Bog-Hag's fen is full of drowned things, and a hag wastes nothing — where a coven has rooted, its Wretches tend to stand between it and the channel, and nobody has asked the hag whether that is by her design. See [[Lore - Hags]].

## 🕯️ Laying one down
A Wretch that is beaten does not die so much as **gutter out**, the place's grip slipping until there is nothing holding the body up. The grave-keepers of the **[[The Cult of the Ashen Veil|Ashen Veil]]** say that is not enough: a Wretch laid down in the place that kept it will get up again, sooner or later, and the only real mercy is to **carry the body out** and give it to fire or to dry earth somewhere the place cannot reach. It is heavy, filthy, unpaid work. The Veil does it anyway — into the Scar itself, where no lawful road runs and nobody pays them to go. They call it **the Long Carry**.

> **The Mote:** In Fenmouth, when the marsh gives back someone the village lost, the guides draw lots for who carries them out. Morgran never draws. He just takes the body into his skiff, and he has never once asked who it was.
