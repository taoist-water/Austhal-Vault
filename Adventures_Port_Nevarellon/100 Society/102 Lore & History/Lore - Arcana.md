# Lore: Arcana
#lore/magic #lore/cosmology #status/draft

> "My lord asked whether the working was real or a trick. I told him yes." — a house reckoner, after a Saltmere supper

> **GM reference.** Ground truth, except where marked as what people say. What the coast actually knows is in the practitioners' theories, the patrons' habits and the street names.

---

## 🔥 What the Arcane is
When the mortals rose against their makers, they **stole the magic and science of their creators** and used it to kill them (see [[Cosmology - The Great Fracture]]). The science survives as a trade: the [[The Guild of Alchemists|Guild of Alchemists]] holds it under seal, split between masters so that no one person carries the whole. The magic did not survive as anything. What was a working whole before the Deicide is now **fragments** scattered across the Disk, and nobody alive has more than a handful.

An Arcanist is someone who has pieced enough of those fragments together to make some of them work. None of them knows why. Each holds a **theory** of what the fragments are and why they answer, and each theory is partly right: right enough to work, wrong enough to fail in ways its holder can't explain.

> **GM-facing.** No practitioner's theory is the truth, and no two agree, because each was built from different fragments of the same stolen whole. A working that fits a practitioner's theory goes cleanly. One that doesn't still works, but it costs them, because they are doing something their own understanding cannot account for. The Arcane has nobody on the other end. A priest borrows from a Domain that is still there and still collecting; an Arcanist uses the stolen tool itself, and the cost lands on the hand that holds it.

## 🪨 The fragments
Practitioners learn from whatever survived. They prize the fragments themselves almost as much as what can be learned from them.
- **Inscriptions.** Tablets, carved lintels, the margins of ruins. The oldest are elven, and the elves who could read them are mostly dead. **Marren Loweb**, one of the Seven, could read the script in the temple under Corvus Spire well enough to know the door could not be shut from this side (see [[The Five Duchies of the Whispering Coast]]).
- **Paintings.** Diagrams on cave walls and barrow stones, older than writing, copied by candlelight and argued over for generations.
- **The bones.** The war-bone that Longbarrow ploughs up every spring, and the petrified remains under the city, still bleeding unanchored energy into the stone. A practitioner who can read a bone's grain can sometimes read what it was for.
- **Quickened pieces.** Wrack that has half-woken. Fey are essence that quickened in still water; these are shards of unanchored wrack that quickened a little and stopped. They turn in the hand, warm to one person and not another, and behave as though they want something. Practitioners call them **quick-things**. They are the rarest fragments and the most argued over, and a practitioner who owns one is rarely the only party with an interest in it.

> **GM-facing.** Quick-things are natural Relics: unique, GM-authored, and each one with a want of its own.

## 📓 The Grimoire
A Grimoire is not a printed book of spells. It is a practitioner's **own working notebook**: rubbings of tablets, copied diagrams, star-tables, the owner's notes in the owner's notation, and pages of failed attempts kept because they were nearly right.
- **Why Arcanists study every night.** A working cannot be memorised the way a song can. It has to be re-derived from the notes, and it holds in the head for about a day before it slips. A practitioner who misses a night can still read straight from the page, slowly and with the book open in one hand.
- **Why one Grimoire is useless to another reader.** The notation is private. A stolen Grimoire is a puzzle in someone else's handwriting, and copying from one is closer to decoding a cipher than to transcribing a text.
- **Vane's Book.** The most copied Grimoire on the coast, attributed to **Vane**, a practitioner from before living memory. Copies have passed hand to hand for generations, each copyist correcting what they thought was wrong, and no two now agree. "Which Vane do you work from?" is a real question, and the answer places a practitioner the way a family name places a stranger.

## 🎩 The kept Arcanist
Wealthy houses keep an Arcanist on staff. The practical reasons are real: someone to read the Low Moons for a voyage, find a seam, ward a strongroom, or say what a dead man's quick-thing is for. But the **social** reason is the one that sets the price. A practitioner in the household, giving a demonstration after supper, is one of the clearest signs on the coast that a house can afford anything.
- **Not an Envoy.** A [[The Golden Company|Golden Company]] Envoy is hired from the Company, which vets them, sets the rate and stands behind them. An Arcanist has nothing behind them, so a patron must **find** one. The prestige is in the finding: in rarity, in reputation, and in lineage ("ours works from a Vane copy two hands removed from the original").
- **The price.** There is no Company to set the rate. A sought-after practitioner costs more than an Envoy's 15–25 gold sovereigns a season, and houses **poach** each other's.
- **Disputations.** A house's Arcanist and a rival house's argue opposing theories before guests, then each does a working to prove their case. Guests wager on the result. It is the coast's most fashionable entertainment that a duke cannot buy outright.
- **Titles.** Households give their Arcanists titles: *Reckoner to the House*, *Philosopher*, *Archmage*. The titles mean nothing outside the house that gave them, and the street knows it. *Archmage* is a vanity title that some forgotten household gave Vane, and it stuck.
- **The houses.** The Valerius and Aerthos courts, which already keep Envoys for show, compete for Arcanists the same way. **Duke Tythius De Vonce** refuses to retain an Envoy on principle, and he would not hire an Arcanist either. His house grew its own: **Imaihil De Vonce** keeps the library at Iron-Spire, corresponds with the Guild's Chapter-House about its moon-tables, and twice has gone to the edge of the Scar to bring back soil and water in stoppered jars (see [[Duchy of De Vonce]]).

## 🤝 Hands and correspondences
There are no colleges, towers or orders of Arcanists, and never have been on this coast.
- **A hand.** One practitioner and one or two wards who copy their master's notation and learn to read it. When the master dies, the wards inherit the Grimoire and spend years arguing over what it meant.
- **A correspondence.** Practitioners who have never met, writing to one another across the coast and beyond it: sharing rubbings, disputing readings, asking whether anyone else has seen what they have seen. A dead practitioner's papers usually include half of somebody else's correspondence.
- **Why no schools.** Because there is no body of knowledge to teach, only notebooks, and because of Corvus. The Undercourt was the nearest thing the coast ever had to a school of the Arcane: a gathering of practitioners around one source, the temple's script, grown large enough to open a door. Since 8 A.A., a practitioner who starts gathering students finds the [[The Cult of the Zenith|Zenith]] asking polite questions about who they are and what they are for. Practising draws no such attention. Gathering does.

## 🧪 Charlatans and demonstration
Most of the "arcanists" in the Muddy Docks are frauds: horoscopes, tonics, a lodestone painted to look like wrack. The public knows it, which is why the real thing **proves itself in public**. A practitioner who wants to be believed does something in front of witnesses, and the best of them have made demonstration an art: the dinner-table working, the disputation, the wager won. Half-awed and half-sceptical is the coast's normal mood towards the Arcane, and practitioners have learned to work it.

## 🧭 The traditions
Every practitioner's theory is their own, but theories cluster, and the street has names for the clusters. Practitioners rarely use them.

| Tradition | Street name | What its practitioners say | Who takes an interest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Astromancy | reckoners | The moons are lamps hung by the makers, and their light still carries the last workings; reckon the angle and you can read it, or borrow it. | Nobody objects. The Guild's observatory does this work under another name. |
| Pyromancy | burners | Fire is not made but **let out**. Every thing holds a latent fire, and a working opens the door it is kept behind. | The Guild's Searchers. Fire without powder cuts into the powder monopoly: a guild quarrel, not a persecution. |
| Necromancy | twitchers | The dead keep a **current** in the bone. War-bone keeps the most, and it can be made to run again. | [[The Cult of the Ashen Veil|The Ashen Veil]], openly. |
| Transmutation | humourists | Every substance is a balance of humours. Rebalance it and it becomes something else. | [[The Tidespoken Clergy|The Tidespoken]], who see the hags' trade in it. |
| Shadow Sorcery | glass-readers | A reflection is a second thing, not a copy, and it can be made to act. | Nobody objects. The [[The Cult of the Crooked Coin|Crooked Coin]] keeps an eye on the good ones. |
| Demonology & Void | namers | A thing's true name is its handle. Name it rightly and it must answer. | Everyone who remembers Corvus. The one tradition still carrying a stigma. |
| Witch Magic & Hedge Craft | cunning folk | Like answers like: a root shaped like a hand heals hands, and a likeness can be made to suffer for its original. Much of it was learned from hags, for a price. | The Tidespoken, for the same reason as Transmutation. |
| Shamanism | — | Not written down. Carried in song and skin-mark by the people of the high Spine, who lived there before the settlers (see [[The Cult of the Rime-Fang]]). Book-learned practitioners envy it and misread it. | Nobody. Few outside the Spine know it exists. |

> **GM-facing.**
> - **Shadow Sorcery** works because a reflection is still, faintly, the province of the **Two-Faced**, the Creator of reflection (see [[Cosmology - The Celestial Graveyard and The war of Creation]]). Glass-readers do not know this.
> - **Demonology** works by naming, and naming was the original wound: the Naming gave the nameless entities their identities. The namers are repeating the first crime in miniature, and the Undercourt took it to its end.
> - **Necromancy's** current is real; it is the essence still bleeding from unanchored war-bone.

## ⚖️ The Arcane and the law
The **Edicts of Armament** say nothing about Grimoires, and no magistrate has yet tried to make them (see [[Law - The Council's Edicts]]). An Arcanist walks the city as freely as anyone else of their class. A kept Arcanist walks it a good deal more freely, with a house's name behind them.

> **The Mote:** Every practitioner on the coast is working from someone else's handwriting, and every one of them adds a page. Nobody has ever burned a Grimoire they inherited. They argue with it instead.
