## 📊 Faction Profile
- **Formal Name:** The Golden Company
- **Street Names:** The Gold-Cloaks, The Company, Marco's Halberds
- **Type:** Chartered Mercenary Company / Standing Military-Police Force, bound to Port Nevarellon under the 99-Year Contract
- **Base of Operations:** **The Sovereign's Gate** — the fortified garrison and toll-keep commanding the entrance to [[The Great Anchor Basin|The Great Anchor Basin]]
- **Estimated Numbers:** ~1,200 sworn Company soldiers, plus roughly 400 [[Faction - The Civic Constabulary (The Coppers)|Blue-Cloak Watch]] operating beneath them as a subordinate auxiliary

---

## ⚔️ Force Structure & Logistics (The Realism Anchor)
The Golden Company is not a single homogenous army — it is two forces wearing one cloak, and the seam between them is where most of its real vulnerabilities live.

- **The Officer Corps:** A comparatively small core of foreign-born veterans, originally shipped in from a distant continental power to break the royal siege during the Civil War. They are the "elite halberdiers" who form the second wave in any riot, hold the High-Steel monopoly on the finest equipment in the city, and remain formally loyal to the Company's overseas Governing Charter rather than to Port Nevarellon itself.
- **The Rank and File:** The bulk of the Company's numbers are locally recruited. At **3 silver pieces a day plus guaranteed rations and a dry cot**, Company service is the single best-paying legal work available to an Un-Landed local — better than any dock wage, and the reason recruitment posts outside the Basin gates are never short of desperate applicants. This means the majority of the soldiers enforcing the Council's Edicts against the Muddy Docks were, a few years prior, standing in the Muddy Docks themselves.
- **The Third Track — Specialists:** A small number of soldiers from either tier are pulled aside for independent, client-facing specialist service. See **The Second Contract**, below.
- **Sources of Income:** The annual Contract Sum paid by the [[Council of Five|Council of Five]], drawn directly from the city's toll and tariff structure — the Vagrancy Tolls, the Basin's cargo tariffs, and a mandatory cut of every **Golden Writ (Peace-Bond)** registered in the city. Confiscated High-Steel and illegal alchemical weapons are auctioned through licensed channels rather than fenced quietly, giving the Company a legitimate secondary revenue stream the Coppers' petty confiscation racket can't match. The Second Contract (below) is a third, separate income stream entirely.
- **Expenses & Upkeep:** Grunt wages, officer pay (a portion of which is remitted back to the Charter-House overseas), and maintenance of the city's hard defenses — the Sea-Wall, the Tide-Wards, and the limestone curtain wall. Defense funding is prioritized toward the Basin and the High Quarter, where the highest-value cargo and the Council's own patrons live; this is the direct cause of the curtain wall's poor condition near the Foundry Slips noted in [[Port Nevarellon]] — it isn't neglect, it's a budget decision.
- **Primary Assets:** The Sovereign's Gate itself, a small fleet of harbor-patrol and breakwater-defense vessels (coastal craft, not a blue-water navy — the Company has no standing presence beyond Port Nevarellon's own waters), and the city's sole legal stockpile of refined High-Steel arms.

## 🎖️ Ranks & Specialist Roles

**The Standing Garrison Track** (99-Year Contract duty):
- **Halberdier / Grunt** — rank and file; overwhelmingly locally-raised.
- **Corporal** — junior NCO.
- **Sergeant** — senior NCO; runs a patrol or checkpoint detail. *(Wren Cobb's rank.)*
- **Lieutenant** — junior officer; foreign-born by strong convention.
- **Captain** — commands a detachment or the Basin garrison shift.
- **Provost** — singular post; oversees internal discipline and Constabulary conduct citywide, answering directly to the High Captain. *(Halvard Stross's rank.)*
- **High Captain** — singular post; the Company's senior field commander in Port Nevarellon. *(Marco's rank.)*

**The Envoy Track (The Second Contract):**
- **Envoy** — a soldier from either tier, selected and separately trained for independent, client-facing specialist work.
- **Senior Envoy** — an Envoy with an established client history and wider authorized-negotiation limits.
- **First Envoy** — singular post; head of the Envoy Corps, sets standards, personally vets which private contracts the Company accepts, answers to the High Captain. *(Isolde Vantry's rank.)*

## 🤝 The Second Contract: The Envoy Corps

Separate from the 99-Year Contract entirely, the Golden Company maintains a small roster of **Envoys** — soldiers pulled from the ranks and given extensive additional training in etiquette, protocol, negotiation, and formal dueling forms, then hired out on individual, privately-negotiated contracts to wealthy persons, guild-masters, and nobility. Where the main Contract binds the Company to the Council of Five, the Second Contract binds an individual Envoy to an individual client — a wholly separate legal and financial arrangement, priced in gold rather than silver. Street cynics call them **the Rented Nobility**.

- **Estimated Numbers:** Fewer than thirty active Envoys at any time — a tiny, hand-picked fraction of the ~1,200 sworn soldiers.
- **Selection & Training:** Nominally open to any Company soldier, officer or rank-and-file, who passes a brutal selection process. In practice the Officer Corps dominates the roster, since foreign-born officers already arrive with some court exposure. A locally-raised Envoy is rare enough that each one is treated as something close to a legend among the Un-Landed — proof, however small, that the ladder isn't fully sealed.
- **Personal Bodyguard:** The most common engagement. Envoys are specifically valued over ordinary hired muscle because they're trained to neutralize a threat *without* causing a scene at a High Quarter dinner — protecting a client's safety and their composure in the same motion.
- **Duel Seconds & Formal Resolution:** Two Landed citizens holding valid Golden Writs may resolve a dispute — a debt, a contract breach, an insult — through Company-witnessed single combat. An Envoy oversees the terms, confirms both Writs are current, and certifies the outcome. A magistrate accepts an Envoy's sworn account of a duel at face value, which is precisely what clients are paying for: not just the fight, but the certainty of never having to prove self-defense in court afterward under [[Law - The Council's Edicts|the Council's Edicts]].
- **Hostile Negotiation Substitution:** A client who doesn't want to personally sit across a table from the [[The Iron-Anchor Syndicate|Iron-Anchor Syndicate]], a rival Duchy, or worse sends an Envoy instead — someone trained to hold a room, and expendable in a way the client is not.
- **The Writ of Voice:** Any negotiating Envoy carries a Council-notarized document — a **Writ of Voice** — defining precisely what they are and are not authorized to agree to on the client's behalf: spending caps, territorial limits, walk-away conditions. It functions as the client's leash on their own representative as much as anyone else's.
- **The Status Symbol:** Some clients retain an Envoy indefinitely and barely use them for anything at all — the Envoy's presence at a dinner table is the point. Duchy Valerius and Duchy Aerthos are the Company's most reliable standing-retainer clients for exactly this reason; [[Tythius De Vonce|Duke Tythius De Vonce]] refuses to retain one on principle, viewing it as an admission that his own household knights aren't enough.
- **Pricing:** A standing retainer runs roughly **15–25 gold sovereigns per season**, paid directly to the Company, with a specialist premium passed to the Envoy above standard Company wage. A single contracted engagement — one negotiation, one duel — runs **2–5 gold sovereigns** depending on risk and duration. This sits squarely in Golden-Writ territory: a service built for people to whom five gold sovereigns is not a meaningful sum.
- **Vetting & Conflict of Interest:** The First Envoy, with the High Captain's final sign-off, screens every prospective client and contract to keep an Envoy from ending up personally bound to someone whose interests could someday cut against the Council's. The system has never yet been tested by a client whose interests turn directly against the Company's own city Contract — which is precisely why nobody in the Company likes to talk about what happens when it is.

## ⚖️ The Contract & Power Dynamics
- **The 99-Year Contract:** Signed in the immediate aftermath of the Civil War, within the living memory of [[Tythius De Vonce|Duke Tythius De Vonce]], one of its original signatories. Roughly **58 years have passed** — meaning **around 41 years remain** on the term. This is a real countdown, not an abstraction: it's the clock [[Garrick the Keelhauler|Garrick]] is quietly betting he can outlast, corrupt, or render irrelevant before it runs out.
- **Political Leverage:** The Contract is woven into the Ducal Accord itself. The Council of Five cannot legally dissolve it without threatening the entire post-Civil-War settlement — the same settlement that keeps the five Dukes from reclaiming the city and keeps the word "King" illegal on the coast. Ending the Contract early isn't a policy choice; it's a constitutional crisis.
- **Monopoly / Unique Value:** The only legally sanctioned standing military force inside Port Nevarellon, and — alongside nobles and sanctioned guild-masters — one of the only parties permitted to purchase refined **High-Steel** under [[Law - The Council's Edicts|the Council's Edicts]]. No other force in the city can match their equipment.
- **Vulnerabilities:**
  - **Fiscal dependency:** the Contract Sum is drawn from the same trade economy the [[The Iron-Anchor Syndicate|Iron-Anchor Syndicate]] depends on. A serious disruption to Port Nevarellon's trade doesn't just hurt merchants — it threatens the Company's own pay.
  - **The Chalced Question:** the Contract's founding purpose was suppressing a *domestic* return to kingship. Whether it legally obligates the Company to fight a full-scale invasion by the exiled [[100 Society/The Chalced Kingdom|Chalced]] royal line is genuinely unsettled — a gap High Captain Marco is quietly aware of and deeply unsettled by (hence his watching "the northern sky").
  - **The Recruitment Fault Line:** as more of the rank and file are raised from the very districts they're ordered to police, quiet reluctance and small mercies are becoming more common lower in the ranks than the Officer Corps would like to admit.
  - **Divided Loyalties in the Envoy Corps:** an Envoy under long-term private retainer answers, in practice, to a client's household as much as to the Company chain of command. Vetting reduces the risk of a genuine conflict-of-interest crisis, but hasn't eliminated it.

## 🧠 Psychology & Internal Culture
- **Contract Before Cause:** The Company's ethos is transactional, not ideological. It doesn't care about street-level crime in the docks; it cares about tax revenue, harbor defense, and making sure the Contract is paid on time. This is a professional army administering a business arrangement, not a crusading order — which is exactly why it can be *fooled* by a good forgery rather than only fought.
- **Unwitting Tool of the Cobalt Feather:** The Company's greatest quiet vulnerability isn't force, it's paperwork. [[The Cobalt Syndicate|The Cobalt Feather Syndicate]] forges manifests convincing enough that Golden Company guards personally escort smuggled cargo out of the Basin, believing it belongs to the Council itself — dramatic irony the Company has no idea it's living inside.
- **Contempt for the Coppers:** Officers routinely override Constabulary arrests and treat Blue-Cloak watchmen as undisciplined amateurs, a resentment the Coppers return in full (see [[Faction - The Civic Constabulary (The Coppers)]]).
- **Envy of the Gilded Roster:** Rank-and-file grunts watching an Envoy step out of a lacquered carriage in tailored silk, on Company coin, breeds real resentment — especially when that Envoy came from the same docks they did. The Officer Corps carries a quieter version of the same resentment, less about money and more about a locally-raised soldier holding the Corps' most prestigious specialist rank.
- **The Slow Drift:** Off the record, veteran NCOs raised locally increasingly describe their loyalty as being to "the cot and the coin," not the Charter-House overseas — a fault line the Officer Corps polices carefully, since a rank-and-file that stops seeing itself as foreign is a rank-and-file that might stop enforcing edicts against its own family.

## 🔗 Key Figures & Relations
- **[[High Captain Marco|High Captain Marco]]:** *(NPC)* The senior Company officer stationed in Port Nevarellon and its de facto local commander. Foreign-born, coldly professional, and unusually strategically minded for a garrison officer — he tracks the Corvus Scar and the northern passes with real anxiety, aware the Contract was never designed for a low-frequency incursion, only for policing merchants and mobs.
- **[[Provost Halvard Stross|Provost Halvard Stross]]:** *(NPC)* Marco's officer responsible for overseeing the Civic Constabulary's conduct citywide — the man Captain Elias "Half-Step" Vance dreads and quietly bribes information toward, terrified Stross will eventually notice how selectively Vance's Toll-House makes its arrests.
- **[[First Envoy Isolde Vantry|First Envoy Isolde Vantry]]:** *(NPC)* Head of the Envoy Corps; Sunken Ward-born and the first — still rare — locally-raised Envoy to reach the top of the specialist track. Moves fluidly between High Quarter drawing rooms and the docks she came from, trusted by neither world quite completely.
- **[[Sergeant Wren Cobb|Sergeant Wren Cobb]]:** *(NPC)* A locally-raised NCO who grew up in the Muddy Docks before taking the Company's coin. Competent, quietly conflicted, and one of the few Company soldiers who still has family in the district she's ordered to police — a small, human counterweight to the Company's institutional coldness.
