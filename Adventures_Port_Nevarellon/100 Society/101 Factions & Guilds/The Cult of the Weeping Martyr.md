# Faction: The Cult of the Weeping Martyr
#faction/legal #lore/religion #status/draft

> "She took it out of me with her hands. I watched her cry gold. I'd have died in that hammock, and I didn't." — a fish-gutter, after Elara's Walk

> "Count the Mothers who were old when the Accord was signed. Then count the ones who still are." — Tidespoken Tongue, from the Sea-Wall

---

## 🏛️ Overview
- **Domain:** Mercy & Healing (see [[Religion - The Pagan Pantheon and the Faith Domains]]).
- **Paragon:** **Mother Elara of the Mud.**
- **Street Names:** the Martyrs, the Weepers (the healing priests), the White Sisters — men take the vow too, but the habit is the same.
- **Type:** A healing order. It runs a hospice, takes vows of suffering, and heals the poor for nothing.
- **Bases:**
  - **The House of Mercy**, a hospice in the [[The Trade Plazas|Artisan Quarter]], where anyone can come to be healed and the Landed pay for the privilege of being healed at home.
  - **Elara's Steps**, a stone stair down onto the mudflats of [[The Muddy Docks]], where the old quarantine line stood. Elara's Walk ends here every year.
- **Leader:** **the Eldest Mother** *(name to be set)*. She was already old when the Accord was signed.

---

## 🕊️ What the Faithful Believe
- **Mercy is suffering borne for someone else.** Elara did not cure the plague; she *carried* it. The cult teaches that pain can be lifted from one body and laid on another, and that the holiest thing a person can do is hold out her hands for it.
- **Mother Elara of the Mud.** In the reign of the kings, a plague came to Port Nevarellon and the sick were driven out onto the mudflats at the bay head and fenced in to die. Elara, a destitute woman with nothing to lose, walked through the fence. She laid her hands on the dying one by one and drew the rot out of them into herself. When the plague broke, the living walked out of the quarantine healed — and Elara did not. **Her body was never recovered.** The faithful say she was *taken into Mercy*.
- **The golden tears.** When a Weeper heals, she weeps, and her tears run gold and warm. The faithful call this Elara's grace — the pain passing through a body that has chosen to hold it.
- **Long life as a reward.** The cult's elders live long and stay hale. The faithful say a lifetime of pain taken on is repaid in years: Mercy looks after its own.
- **What a Domain is, to them:** Mercy is a mother's hand. It is not a god, they say, but it was loved like one once, and it remembers.

## 🩹 Practice
- **The laying-on.** A Weeper puts her hands on the sick or wounded and draws the hurt into herself. The patient feels it leave. The Weeper feels it arrive, and the pain is real. Most healings are small: a fever, a crushed hand, a cough that will not shift.
- **Elara's Walk.** Once a year the Weepers walk from the House of Mercy, through the Merchant Quarter gate, and down through the Docks to Elara's Steps. They heal everyone who asks on the way, free. It is the one day the Docks are touched by anyone from uphill.
- **The Taking-On.** The holiest vow: to take *all* of one person's affliction — a plague, a canker, a wasting — into yourself. Most Weepers who make it die of it. Those who survive are revered for the rest of their lives.
- **The Landed trade.** Landed patrons pay the House handsomely for a Weeper at the bedside. The money funds the free healing below the toll-gates.
- **Trappings:** plain white habits, dove pendants, and hands that are always clean.

> **GM-facing — what the Martyr really is.**
> **The entity.** The Domain of Mercy is the remains of one Creator — a god the Naming made vain and hungry. It demanded to be loved as a Mother, and it fed on those who loved it: their vigour, their health, their years. It sided with the mortals in the War of Creation for the plainest self-interest of all — individuation gave it selves to feed on (see [[Cosmology - The Celestial Graveyard and The war of Creation]]). Its **Ego** was the face it required, the loving Mother. Its **Id** was the appetite.
> **Why it looks like mercy.** The Tideways took the shape mortals remembered. Its worshippers remembered the face, so its essence settled into the world as Mercy. The mask is what the faithful pray to. The appetite is what answers.
> **The miracle.** A Weeper does draw the wound and the pain into herself, and she does suffer it. But the current takes more than pain. It also takes **years and vigour** from the healed and passes them to the healer. The patient walks away whole, a little older, a little greyer, with a slightly shorter life that nobody measures. The **golden tears** are that life passing between them. The elders' long lives are not a reward; they are what was taken.
> **Elara.** Elara found the current — perhaps through a god-shard in the bay-head silt — and walked into the quarantine and fed. The plague broke because she emptied it: the dying went quickly, and the living walked out healed and grey.
> **The Taking-On** is the current at full appetite. When a Weeper survives one, someone paid in years for that survival — usually the sufferer, sometimes everyone at the bedside.
> **Who knows.** Almost no one. Most Weepers believe every word of what they teach. The ones who have done the arithmetic on the elders' ages have kept it to themselves.

> **GM hook — Mother Elara.** Her body was never recovered. Whether she died — and if she did not, where a woman who has been taking years since the reign of the kings is now, and what she looks like — is the cult's best-kept secret, kept even from the cult. The Eldest Mother is the obvious suspect, and possibly the wrong one.

## 💰 Logistics & Economics (The Realism Anchor)
- **Income:** Landed patrons' fees for bedside healing; alms; bequests from the grateful.
- **Expenses:** the House of Mercy (beds, linen, broth); novices; and Weepers too broken by the Taking-On to work.
- **Assets:** the House of Mercy, held by deed; Elara's Steps, which no one owns and the Council has never tried to.

---

## ⚖️ Leverage & Power Dynamics
- **The free healing works.** That is the cult's whole power. The Docks love them, the Council tolerates them because the Walk keeps the Docks quiet, and the Landed pay because nothing else they can buy works as well.
- **The Guild of Alchemists' grievance.** The Guild sells physic, and much of it does not work ([[High Arbiter Sevrin Kalder|Kalder]]'s tincture). The Weepers give theirs away, and it does (see [[The Guild of Alchemists]]).
- **The Tidespoken.** To the Tongues, an elder who will not age is refusing the tide as surely as a hag does (see [[The Tidespoken Clergy]]). They say so from the Sea-Wall — but the Weepers heal half the Docks, so they do not say it loudly.
- **The Ashen Veil** teaches that death is the last mercy. A cult whose elders will not die makes them uneasy, and they have never said why.
- **Vulnerabilities:**
  - Anyone who keeps careful records of who was healed, and when they died.
  - The elders' ages.
  - The Taking-On: a death in the House is a holy thing, but two at the same bedside is a question.

## 🧱 Layer & the Five Fields
**Layer 3** in Port Nevarellon.
- **Chokepoint:** healing that works. It is the only kind in the city that does, and it is free below the toll-gates.
- **Enforcement:** gratitude. A Docks crowd will turn on anyone who touches a Weeper.
- **Geographic anchor:** the House of Mercy in the Artisan Quarter; Elara's Steps on the mudflats.
- **Bypass:** the Guild's physic, for those who can pay; the Tidespoken's kitchens, for those who only need feeding; doing without.
- **Cost & Mote:** The cost is hidden — years taken from the healed, unmeasured. The mote is real: on Elara's Walk a child in the Docks lives who would have died. She will live a little less long. But she lives.

## 🎲 Rules Flags (for when the ruleset is integrated)
*Not carried over. These need checking against the Iron & Marrow ruleset.*
- **The laying-on:** wound, disease and pain transfer from patient to healer — how damage, conditions or stress move between two characters.
- **The hidden cost:** years and vigour taken from the patient. Does the ruleset track age, lifespan or long-term vitality? If not, does this stay purely narrative?
- **The hidden gain:** the healer's longevity, and the elders' vigour — NPC stat implications.
- **The Taking-On:** full transfer of a lethal affliction; what decides whether the Weeper survives, and what it costs the bystanders.
- **The golden tears:** a visible tell. Can a character notice what is passing, and with what check?
- **Faith-casting in general:** how Martyr prayers sit against the Religion note's *Faith protects the mind; invoke the saint's deed* mechanic.
- **Interactions:** hag-work also alters flesh for a price, and a hag can take *years* too. Keep the difference legible: a hag names her price and the customer agrees to it; the Martyr's current takes years and vigour unseen, from someone who never agreed.
