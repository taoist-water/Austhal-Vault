# Faction: The Guild of Alchemists
#faction/legal #status/draft

> "I coopered their kegs for twenty years and never once saw what went into them. That isn't secrecy, lad. That's a trade." — Ottavian Kress, the Water Seat

> "The Guild'll sell you the dark and the lamp in the same week." — Muddy Docks saying

---

## 📊 Faction Profile
- **Formal Name:** The Guild of Alchemists
- **Street Names:** the Guild. Its senior masters are **the Sealed**. Its nitre-diggers are **the saltpetre-men**, and nobody says that kindly.
- **Type:** A cross-border chartered guild and trade-secret cartel. It keeps chapter-houses in every realm that buys its powder, and it has no capital.
- **Port Nevarellon chapter:** a Layer 3 body with its hands on Layer 1 chokepoints. It has four sites in the city, and its nitre-yards outside the wall (see *The Powder Chain*):
  - **The Chapter-House**, on the seaward edge of [[The High Quarter]]'s cliffs. It holds the licence office and the Guild court, and on its roof the **observatory and instrument room** where the Low Moons tables are reckoned (see [[Framework - The Coastal Reckoning]]).
  - **The Powder Hulk**, a dismasted hull moored in the outer harbour off the Sea-Wall, away from anything that burns. All legal powder in the city is stored here and comes ashore by the keg, under a Searcher's tally.
  - **[[The Brine-Glow Depot]]** in [[The Muddy Docks]], the lantern workshop.
  - **Licensed refineries** in [[The Foundry Slips]]. Some are the Guild's own; others are worked by licensees under Guild inspection.
- **Estimated Numbers (Port Nevarellon):** a few dozen masters, perhaps two hundred journeymen and apprentices, and several hundred licensed hands, lamp-tenders and diggers who are not members at all.
- **Chapter head:** the **Chapter-Warden** *(name to be set)*.
- **Named members:** [[Halcus Rive]] *(no file yet)*, the chapter's assessor, who licenses Brine-Glow lanterns.

## 🏛️ Structure: No Capital, No Throat
- **No mother-house.** The Guild is governed by **the Convocation**, a gathering of masters from every chapter. It meets every few years at a chapter drawn by lot. No realm can seize the Guild by seizing a city, because there is no city where the whole of it sits. This is deliberate. Every body that everyone courts is a body someone eventually tries to own.
- **One charter per realm.** Each chapter holds a charter from the power it sits under. The Port Nevarellon chapter's charter was granted by the [[Council of Five|Council]] and is lodged in the Register. That makes it voidable by the [[The Cult of the Zenith|Cult of the Zenith]], which can never confirm it. But anyone who moves to strike it must first pay arbitration fees scaled to the value in dispute, and here the value is the city's whole legal powder supply. Nobody has tried. (The same doctrine protects the Wondrous Markets — see [[Reference - The Wondrous Markets]].)
- **No Council seat, by choice.** The chapter has been offered a seat and has refused it. A seat would make the Guild a party to the Ducal Accord and to every quarrel at the Council table, and its neutrality is what it sells.
- **The ladder:**
  1. **Apprentice.** Admitted by examination.
  2. **Journeyman.** Mixes, corns and tends under a master's eye.
  3. **Master.** Landed under the chapter's charter. May buy High-Steel as a sanctioned guild-master (see [[Law - The Council's Edicts]]).
  4. **Sealed Master.** Sworn to the Seal, and one of the few who know how the salt is made.
- **The Guild court.** Members answer to the Guild for breaches of the Seal, not to the High Courts. The Council allows this because the alternative is a powder-maker giving evidence in open court.
- **The Searchers.** The Guild's inspectors. In the city they have the right to inspect any licensed magazine, keg, lantern or refinery. Outside it, they hunt the Unsealed.

## 🔒 The Seal: How the Secret Is Kept
- **What the secret is.** Anyone can buy sulphur and charcoal. The secret is **the white salt** — saltpetre: how to grow it, how to draw it, and how to refine it clean. The second secret is the **corning**, the working of the mixture into grains that keep in a ship's hold without separating.
- **Split knowledge.** Chapters mix and corn their powder from salt that arrives already refined, under seal. Journeymen know the proportions. Masters know the corning. Only the Sealed know how the salt is made, and no chapter keeps a written account of it.
- **The cage.** A Sealed Master may not leave the Guild's service. The Guild pays them well and honours them: masters are Landed, and their children marry into Landed houses. A master who walks out is **Unsealed**, and the Searchers come for them. The Guild does not publish what happens next.
- **Rogue alchemists** come in two kinds:
  - **The Unsealed.** Defectors who know part of the process and are hunted for it. [[Silas Bane]]'s rogue cell is drawn from *rogue members of the Port Nevarellon chapter* — Unsealed in all but name, and the Searchers are working through the chapter's lists.
  - **Hedge-chemists.** Never admitted. They buy god-shards from the [[The Cobalt Feather Syndicate|Cobalt Feather]] and [[Sun-Iron]] from the [[The Iron-Anchor Syndicate|Iron-Anchor]], and make things that sometimes work. None of them makes powder that keeps.

## ⛏️ The Powder Chain (The Realism Anchor)
- **Saltpetre.** Grown in the Guild's **nitre-yards** outside the landward curtain wall, downwind of the Foundry Slips — long heaps of dung, lime, ash and piss, turned and watered for a year or more before they yield. The work is filthy and the Guild staffs it with bondsmen: it buys up Debt-Bonds (see [[Law - The Council's Edicts]]) and works them off in the yards.
- **The Right of the Spade.** Every Guild charter on the coast carries it. A saltpetre-man may enter any stable, byre, dovecote or cellar in the chartering realm and dig out the nitre-rich earth, and the owner may not refuse. It is the one grievance the Muddy Docks and the High Quarter share.
- **Sulphur.** Bought from the [[The Five Duchies of the Whispering Coast|Corvus Scar]]'s sulphur seeps, from Scar-Holders who hold no deed and pay no Duke's levy on it. It is the only trade in coin the Scar has. The Guild prefers sellers with no Duke, so it quietly prefers the Scar unannexed.
- **Charcoal.** Burned in the De Vonce oak stands.
- **Kegs.** Coopered only under Guild licence — the contract the Coopers' Guild cannot afford to lose (see [[Ottavian Kress]]).
- **Storage.** The Powder Hulk. Nothing is kept ashore beyond a day's working stock.

## 💰 What the Guild Sells
- **Powder and licences.** Under the Powder Edict every legal measure in the city is issued, logged and inspected by the chapter, and every charge is drawn under a Guild licence in the bearer's name. [[Garrick the Keelhauler|Garrick]]'s licence is held by a shell company. [[Maeve the Scribe|Maeve]]'s derringer is licensed in Garrick's name.
- **Light.** The [[The Brine-Glow Lanterns|Brine-Glow Lanterns]] are a massive municipal contract (see [[Port Nevarellon]]). The algae is harvested at **Saltlight** on the Shield Atolls and prepared at the Depot. Halcus Rive licenses every lantern.
- **Brine-Fire.** Licensed stock only. Before every Low Moons the chapter performs **the Shuttering**: every measure of Brine-Fire in the city is sealed and logged until the moons rise again.
- **The Low Moons tables.** Reckoned in days at the Chapter-House and sold. The Tidespoken give their warnings away free at the Sea-Wall, so the poor are warned last, but they are warned.
- **Physic and glass.** Lenses ([[Lucia Marrenhal]]'s, at ruinous cost) and tinctures ([[High Arbiter Sevrin Kalder]]'s, which does not work).
- **Shard-assay.** The licensed assay and working of god-shards. This is the monopoly the Cobalt Feather's customers are paying to get around.

## ⚖️ Leverage & Power Dynamics
- **Neutrality means it sells to everyone.** In the Civil War the Guild sold powder to both sides and was paid by both. [[Tythius De Vonce]] was there, and remembers.
- **Its weapon is refusal.** A chapter can shut its magazine. In Port Nevarellon the threat has always been enough.
- **Everyone courts it.** The Duchies want preferential terms, the Council wants the lantern contract cheaper, and every Layer 3 crook wants a licence in a clean name.
- **Tuwal Ghorun.** The Guild keeps a chapter in the southern port-cities and sells to [[The Twelgorn Kingdom|the realm]]: blasting powder for the slave-mines, and charges for the steppe-lords' pistols.

> **GM-facing.** The Guild's Low Moons tables give the same date the bound god gave its realm: the Low Moons falling in the Opening, around **59 A.A.** For years the southern orders have been rising toward that date, and the Convocation has noticed. It has told no one, because telling would be taking a side. If the invasion comes, the Guild will have armed it and profited from the warning it withheld.

- **Vulnerabilities:**
  - **The Unsealed.** Every defector is part of the secret walking loose.
  - **Silas's lantern plot.** A rogue cell making synthetic Brine-Glow fluid attacks the one contract that makes the chapter popular.
  - **The Right of the Spade.** It makes the Guild hated by people who would otherwise have no opinion of it.
  - **Its own stock.** The Low Moons make Undertow-touched alchemical stock unstable. The magazine the Guild controls becomes the Guild's liability for nine to twelve nights at a time.
  - **Being worth seizing.** A realm that took a chapter would get a hulk, a magazine and some apprentices. It would not get the salt, and it would never buy powder again. The Guild is betting that every ruler can do that arithmetic.

## 🧱 Layer & the Five Fields
The Port Nevarellon chapter is **Layer 3** by reach. It holds two **Layer 1 chokepoints** outright — the city's light and its legal powder — and has the strongest claim to a third, the calendar, which [[Framework - The Coastal Reckoning]] still lists as unassigned. The parent Guild sits **outside the Layers**.
- **Chokepoint:** the white salt.
- **Enforcement:** the licence (the Powder Edict gives the chapter's paper the force of law), the Searchers, and refusal to sell.
- **Geographic anchor:** in the city, the Chapter-House observatory and the Powder Hulk. The parent Guild has none, by design.
- **Bypass:** Unsealed masters; hedge-chemists buying shards and Sun-Iron; the Tidespoken's free tables.
- **Cost & Mote:** The cost is the bondsmen in the nitre-yards, the Right of the Spade, and the poor warned last. The mote is that **the Guild is the one door in Port Nevarellon that opens by examination**, not by birth or money. A Sunken Ward child who can reckon the moons can sit the test. The door never opens outward again, but it opens. And the Guild keeps its maimed: a burned journeyman is kept on as a tallyman, and a master's widow draws a pension.

## 🔎 Design Note: Real-World Models (GM-facing)
- **The Hanseatic League:** cross-border chapters under local charters; refusal to trade as the weapon.
- **Byzantine Greek fire:** a secret split so no one person holds it.
- **The Murano glassmakers:** masters honoured, enriched and forbidden to leave.
- **The English saltpetremen:** the Right of the Spade.
- **The Knights Templar:** what happens to a neutral body everyone owes.
- City guilds (the Coopers, the Shipwrights, the Victuallers) should follow **medieval craft guilds** instead: monopoly by charter, the right of search, and apprentice ladders. They are local, not cross-border.
