## 📐 What This Document Is For
How the city's craft and trade guilds work, and which ones exist. These are **local bodies chartered by the [[Council of Five|Council]]**. [[The Guild of Alchemists]] is cross-border and works differently — see its note.

Build every future city guild against this note. A guild gets its own file only when play needs it; until then, its row in the roster below is its canon.

---

## ⚖️ Two Kinds of Charter
[[Law - The Council's Edicts]] makes a citizen Landed if they hold "a Guild Charter: the registered paperwork for a recognized business." In practice there are two instruments, and the gap between them is where the city's labour politics live.

1. **The guild charter** — the body's. It is issued by the Council and counter-copied in the Register. At the Council's **First Sitting** in 0 A.A., every guild then trading in the city had its charter re-issued on a single instrument: **the Charter Roll**. A new guild charter creates a new class of Landed persons at a stroke, so the Council treats it as a change to the Register — an **Instrument of the Whole**, needing four of five. **No new city guild has been chartered since the Accord.** The roster is the Accord's roster.
2. **The shop charter** — the master's. This is the "registered paperwork for a recognized business" the Law names, and it makes its holder Landed. The guild sells it, the Council stamps it, and the Register keeps the counter-copy. Each guild decides how many it sells, which is how masters keep masters few. Anyone may buy one in principle — [[First Envoy Isolde Vantry]] is saving for one.

The [[The Golden Company|Golden Company]]'s charter was recognised under the Accord as equivalent to a guild charter, which is why its officers are **Landed by Commission**.

**The [[The Cult of the Zenith|Zenith]] can void either kind of charter and confirm neither.**

**The consequence:** whole trades — fish-gutters, net-menders, carvers, mud-dredgers — have no guild of their own, and under the four-of-five rule they never will. They sell under someone else's stamp.

## 🏪 Owning Is Not Selling
**The deed lets you own. The stamp lets you sell.** A guild controls the *making and selling of its craft's goods*, not enterprise in general. The stamp follows the goods, not the seller. There are four ways to do business in Port Nevarellon:

| | What it takes | Examples | What it can and can't do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Guild business** | A shop charter (Landed) | Coopers, shipwrights, smiths, the food-trade charters | Makes and stamps guild goods; stamps Basin work-tokens; sponsors its workers' grievances; buys High-Steel; has the guild box behind it. In return: guild-set prices and quality, capped numbers, and the right of search |
| **Deed business** | A Council-stamped deed to the premises (Landed) | Taverns, lodging, warehouses, [[The Shades]], lenders and banking houses, carters | Sets its own prices and answers to no guild. **Cannot** make guild goods (it buys them stamped), stamp Basin tokens, or sponsor a worker's grievance — the Law gives that to a *Guild*-Master only |
| **Wholesale & foreign trade** | Keel papers, or a Landed importer; Basin tariffs | [[Palla Vantry]]'s houses, foreign captains, whole cargoes | Stamps govern retail sale inside the walls. Whole cargoes trade under Basin tariffs — which is what makes the city a Wondrous Market |
| **Un-Landed trade** | Nothing legal | Fish-gutters, carvers, midwives, marsh-guides | May sell labour and services freely, and goods only through stamp-rent or illegally. **Cannot enforce a debt in court** — which is why the Syndicate does the collecting in the Docks |

- **The stamp rule binds everyone; the penalty depends on class.** A Landed tavern-keeper caught selling unstamped goods pays the fine and is quietly embarrassed. An Un-Landed seller pays or goes to the debt-prisons. It is the Powder Edict's shape again: the law is the same for everyone and does not land the same on anyone.
- **Deed businesses need guild-masters.** A tavern that wants Basin labour, or wants its barmaid's grievance heard, has to go through one. That is the market [[Ottavian Kress]]'s sponsorship network trades in.
- **Why Garrick wants a charter.** His deeds already let him *own* whatever he likes. What they can't do is employ legally in the Basin, arm his men with High-Steel, or give his dockers a road into court. A guild does all three.

> *Rules-side flag: if the Iron & Marrow ruleset has business-ownership or downtime rules, this guild / deed split bears on them.*

## 🔨 How a City Guild Works
Every city guild does all of these.

- **The stamp.** Under the Guild-Tax, anything sold without a Landed Guild-Master's stamp costs the seller **1 sp** or a term in the debt-prisons (see [[Economy - the Price of Survival]]). So masters rent their stamps. Under **stamp-rent**, the carver's toy and the fisher's catch are sold under a master's wax, for a cut. Most of the Docks' legal trade is somebody else's.
- **The token.** Un-Landed labourers enter [[The Great Anchor Basin]] only with a Guild-stamped daily work-token. At dawn the masters' clerks stamp the day's tokens at the Basin gates — **the token-call**. If no master stamps you, you don't work in the Basin today.
- **Sponsorship.** An Un-Landed grievance reaches the High Courts only through a Landed Guild-Master, framed as "damage to the Master's labor force." [[Ottavian Kress]] built a Council seat on selling this.
- **Search.** A master may enter any member's shop to check goods and weights. The guild seizes bad work and fines the maker itself, before any magistrate hears of it.
- **The ladder:**
  1. **Apprentice.** Bound by a **Bond of Service** lodged in the Register — so an Arbiter can void a cruel indenture, and never confirm a fair one.
  2. **Journeyman.** Un-Landed, paid Guild rates.
  3. **Master.** Holds a shop charter and is Landed. Latinises on climbing, as Kress did.
- **Tools.** Tools of the trade may be carried only at work or on the way to it (the Law's *Guild Confinement*).
- **High-Steel.** Sanctioned guild-masters are among the few permitted to buy it.
- **Halls.** The guild-halls stand in [[The Trade Plazas]] — the "legal guilds" of [[Port Nevarellon]].
- **The guild box.** Dues pay for a member's burial, a widow's allowance and bread for the maimed. The first thing a guild does for its own is bury them. For an Un-Landed worker, Guild rates are the best legal wage in the city after the Company's.

## ⚡ Where the Stories Are
- **Garrick's charter.** It needs four councillors, and any two can block it without giving a reason. If he gets it, it will be the first new guild since the Accord and the first that ever belonged to the Docks: stevedores would become journeymen on Guild rates, with a master to sponsor their grievances. A crook's charter would be the best thing to happen to the Docks in fifty-eight years. That is why Silas hates it, and why the Council fears it.
- **Dead charters (GM-facing).** The Charter Roll lists guilds that have since died out — Corvus trades among them. A charter nobody holds is still on the Register. Nobody has moved to void it, and the Zenith cannot confirm it. Reviving a dead charter bypasses the four-of-five vote entirely. That is a quieter road for Garrick than buying four councillors, and someone may already have found it.
- **Forged wax.** A stamp is wax, and the [[The Cobalt Feather Syndicate|Cobalt Feather]] forges wax for a living. Stamp-rent is a market; a forged stamp undercuts it.
- **The Duchies' levers.** Every guild's supply runs back to a Duchy. De Vonce alone feeds four of them.

## 🧱 Layer & the Five Fields
Each city guild is **Layer 3**. Its master's vote and its councillor give it a hand on **Layer 1** (see [[Framework - The Three Layers]]).
- **Chokepoint:** the stamp.
- **Enforcement:** the Guild-Tax, the Basin token and the debt-prisons; the guild's own search and fines.
- **Geographic anchor:** the guild-halls in the Trade Plazas, and the token-call at the Basin gates.
- **Bypass:** forged stamps; the Union; the unlicensed Docks markets; dead charters.
- **Cost & Mote:** The cost is the trades with no guild, stamp-rent, and the token-call's daily cruelty. The mote is the guild box, the Guild rate, and sponsorship — bought or not, the only door an Un-Landed worker has into a court. Thole's yards employ four hundred Un-Landed at Guild rates. It is not kindness, but it is bread.

## 🔎 Design Note: Real-World Models (GM-facing)
- **Medieval craft guilds and the London livery companies:** monopoly by charter, the right of search, and the freedom of the city through a company.
- **Stamp-rent:** a medieval "foreigner" — anyone without the freedom of the city — could trade only under a freeman's name.
- **The Union:** journeymen's brotherhoods such as the French *compagnonnage*.
- **The token-call:** the dock "call-on", in which labour was hired daily at the dock gates.
- **Owning is not selling:** medieval guilds regulated crafts and retail, not all enterprise; inns, carriers and wholesale merchants often sat outside them. London's "liberties" (Southwark, Blackfriars) lay beyond guild reach altogether — the Muddy Docks behaves like one, except that the Syndicate, not the law, fills the gap.
