## 📐 What This Document Is For

Four rolling tables of 50, for populating the Whispering Coast without drifting off-register. These are **unallocated slots**, not canon — nothing here is bound to a document until it's actually used. Anything drawn from a table and placed in the world should get a canon-tracker entry and a stated parent.

**Nothing in these tables contradicts an existing document.** Where an entry deliberately extends canon (Concord Road waystations, Corvus Scar ruins, Spine Aqueduct settlements), it's marked *→ extension* and noted in the Flags section at the end.

## 🔤 The Naming Grammar (derived from existing canon)

Six registers are already in the vault. They aren't decorative — the register **is** the class marker, and mixing them is how you signal social movement.

| Register | Sound | Canon examples | Who uses it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Coastal Common** | Blunt, 1–2 syllables, hard stops. Surnames are occupational, physical, or geographic. | Wren Cobb, Silas Bane, Kelf Thorne, Halvard Stross, Haren Twarde, Alfric Danniken, Lidda Shoon, Garrick, Maeve, Kaleb | Un-Landed, dockers, marsh-folk, rural tenants, most of the Blue-Cloaks |
| **B — Landed Latinate** | Polysyllabic, vowel-heavy, `-us / -ius / -a / -os` endings. Surnames are house names. | Tythius De Vonce, Valerius, Aerthos, Corvus, Isolde Vantry, Telorna Belaar, Nevarellon | High Quarter, ducal houses, Council patrons, saints |
| **C — Southern (Twelgorn)** | Guttural, `Al-` patronymic particle, long back vowels. | Qasim Al Goor | Twelgorn-born, escaped slaves, Retrievers |
| **D — Dwarven** | Consonant-dense, no soft endings; a trade-lineage word replaces a surname. | *(none in canon — proposed)* | Oakhaven Cove, deep-mine work, the Spine |
| **E — Atoll (Elf / Halfling / mixed)** | Soft, liquid consonants, tidal imagery. **Weathered, not ethereal.** | *(none in canon — proposed)* | Shield Atolls stilt-villages, reef fisher communities |
| **F — Half-Orc / Monstrous descent** | Mac-/Mor- prefixes, hard clusters. Often a single name — chattel status denied them lineage. | Maccorrack, Morgran | Escaped slaves, dock muscle, marsh outcasts |
| **B-e — Elf-descended Landed** | Register B house name, but the *given* name is liquid and multi-vowelled. Marks elven blood the bearer may be trying to downplay. | Sheandri De Vonce, Imaihil De Vonce | Half-elven nobility, chiefly House De Vonce |

**Rule zero — devotional naming.** The commons name children after paragons, softened and lengthened so as not to claim the saint outright. **Vaelen** is not a collision with *Vael the Mute*; Vaelen is a child whose parents kept the Ashen Veil, and the coast hears that the moment it hears the name. This is the single most productive naming engine in the setting: the name states the family's faith before the bearer opens their mouth, and it is *not* revocable when the bearer stops believing.

| Paragon | Domain / Cult | Devotional given names |
|---|---|---|
| Vael the Mute | Death — Ashen Veil | Vaelen, Vaelis, Vaela, Vale, Velin |
| Saint Senecus | Strategy — Iron Horizon | Senec, Senna, Secus, Necca, Senric |
| Aurelius the Architect | Law — Zenith | Aurel, Relius, Orla, Aurick, Reliana |
| Mother Elara of the Mud | Mercy — Weeping Martyr | Elarin, Lara, Elrid, Marra, Elari |
| Kaelen the Survivor | Winter & Wilds — Rime-Fang | Kael, Kaelin, Kaila, Lenna, Kaeric |
| Thalass's Omen | Sea & Storms — Tidespoken | Tallis, Thala, Lassen, Thalin, Tass |
| Rook's Folly | Trickery — Crooked Coin | Rooke, Rookin, Rell, Folly *(as a nickname only)* |

**Two consequences worth using.** A Crooked Coin devotional name is a liability in the High Quarter and a credential in the Docks — the only register that inverts by district. And the High Quarter mostly *doesn't* do this: Register B given names are house-traditional, not devotional, because Landed families name for ancestors they can prove. A Latinate surname attached to a devotional given name (*Vaelis Marrenhal*, *Rooke Ossius*) means a family that climbed within living memory and did not scrub the given name on the way up. Somebody in that house lost that argument.

**Two rules that fall out of the [[History - The Broken Crown of Austhal|Ducal Accord]]:**

1. **The King-Ban applies to place names.** No mortal may hold the moniker "King" on the Whispering Coast. Fifty-odd years on, that means *Kingsford*, *Crown Cross*, and *Royal Quay* don't exist — they were **re-chiselled**. Villages carrying a scraped-off name are common, cheap to render, and instantly legible as post-Accord. Several are seeded in Table 2.

   **Two classes of scraped name, and the difference matters.**
   - **Decree-scraped.** The Council or a ducal clerk struck the old name from the rolls and issued a replacement. Bland, bureaucratic, often literal — *Scrapewell* was *Kingswell* and the new name is just a description of what was done to the old one. Nobody local chose it.
   - **Folk-scraped.** The place renamed itself, usually violently, usually *before* the Accord existed to require it. These names are older than the law that would have compelled them, and the distinction is a matter of fierce local pride. **Marten's Cross** is the type specimen.

   This distinction does real work: it establishes that the King-Ban codified something the commons had already been enforcing on their own. The merchants did not invent the loathing of crowns. They wrote it down and took credit.
2. **A name is upward mobility, and it's visible.** An Un-Landed citizen who buys a deed almost always Latinises. *Cobb* becomes *Cobbius*; *Thorne* becomes *Thornelius*. The High Quarter can hear it happening and finds it hilarious. This is free, permanent social friction — use it.

# Table 2 — Settlements, Holds & Forts (50)

Every entry is placed. **Region** states the parent — no floating villages. **Tier** is the mechanical variable.

### The Tier Variable

| Tier | Label | What's there |
|---|---|---|
| **T0** | Hamlet | A handful of families, maybe a shrine, no real market |
| **T1** | Town | A proper market square, a working forge, a temple or chapter house |
| **T2** | City | A genuine trade hub. Guild halls, multiple forges, a cathedral |
| **T3** | Capital / Metropolis | The seat of power. Anything that exists, exists here |
| **T—** | *(no tier)* | Ruins and abandonments. Nothing is available and nothing is for sale |

**Tier measures services, not headcount.** A four-hundred-soul fishing village with no market and no smith is **T0**. A hundred-and-fifty-soul crossroads with a market day, a forge and a chapel is **T1**. This keeps the tag mechanically useful — it answers *what can be bought, repaired, healed, hired or heard here* — instead of restating a population figure the rules will never consult. Population stays in the prose where it belongs.

**Forts and waystations are tiered by what a traveller can actually obtain.** A ducal garrison with an armourer and a chaplain is **T1** regardless of whether civilians live there; an undermanned watchtower is **T0**.

### 📌 Lore Note — Marten's Cross *(Table 2, #47)*

During the Civil War, a minor lord named **Marten** proclaimed himself King over a holding of perhaps four hundred souls — a crossroads, a mill, and the fields between them — gambling that a crown claimed early would be a crown recognised later, whoever won. He spent the next several seasons asserting that claim on the only people within reach of it. What he did to them is recorded nowhere and remembered precisely.

The townsfolk took him at the marker stone and nailed him to a cross there. Not a gallows — a cross, at the crossroads, at the boundary of the county, where every traveller entering or leaving would have to pass him. The bones are still at the stone.

**Why this is load-bearing:**
- Marten died *before* the Ducal Accord existed. The commons executed a self-made king years before the merchants outlawed the title. The King-Ban is not a merchant invention — it is a merchant **ratification** of something the peasantry had already settled with their hands. Every ducal claim to have granted the Accord's ultimate law gets quietly weaker in light of it.
- The village is not named for a hero. It is named for the man they killed, kept as a warning. That is the correct register for this setting: the mote of hope is that they *acted*; the grimdark is that the monument is a corpse.
- **The bones are maintained.** Fifty-eight years of weather does not leave an articulated skeleton at a roadside. The village re-sets what falls, replaces what the crows take, and does not discuss where the replacements come from. Nobody alive remembers Marten. Everybody maintains him. When a player character finally works out that the bones cannot all be original, that is a scene.

**Hooks:** The Cult of the Zenith has no doctrine for a lynching that predates the law it enforced. The Cult of the Crooked Coin claims Marten as an anti-parable. A ducal clerk has, twice in living memory, tried to have the bones removed as a public-order matter — and been quietly told by his own Duke to let it alone.
