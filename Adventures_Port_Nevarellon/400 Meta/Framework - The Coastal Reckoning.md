# Framework: The Coastal Reckoning
#meta/framework #lore/cosmology #lore/society #status/solid

> "The Guild says the year is eight hundred and forty days long and the Council says it is thirteen turns. Both are paid to be right. My rent is due on whichever one arrives first." — Muddy Docks proverb

---

## 📐 What This Document Is For

The calendar of the Whispering Coast. It exists because [[Religion - The pagan Pantheon and the Faith Domains|the cults]] needed a year to hang their rites on, and because a setting where debts, tolls, writs, and contracts are the primary instruments of oppression cannot function without an agreed answer to *when is it due*.

It is built from three things already in canon and invents no new cosmology:
- The moons are the corpses of secondary creator-entities, each carrying its own soft luminous glow *(`Cosmology - The Celestial Graveyard`)*
- **The Low Moons** are a recurring, trackable alignment, not an omen *(same)*
- **The Winter Moons** are a season, named for moons rather than for weather *(`The Inner Sea`)*

Canon had already decided the coast counts time by moons. This document does the arithmetic.

## ☀️ The Sky — What Actually Happens Up There

**The sun does not move. It stops existing, and then it exists again.**

It has no orbit, no arc, and no track. It fades out of material presence and fades back in, and **it does not return to the place it left.** Mortals do not know the mechanism — no living tradition on Austhal has the concept — and they do not need to, because what they observe is sufficient and unarguable: the light comes back somewhere else.

Its **duration is regular**. Its **position is not**. That single asymmetry is what makes coastal civilisation possible and coastal navigation lethal.

### What this costs

| Lost | Because | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **East, west, and every solar bearing** | The sun rises nowhere in particular | Cardinal direction is **stellar**, never solar |
| **Sundials, solar noon, shadow-reckoning** | Shadows point somewhere different every day | Hours are kept by bell, water-clock, and candle — all of them owned by someone |
| **Oriented architecture** | No fixed sun-side to build toward | Streets follow water and terrain. Port Nevarellon has no grid and never could |
| **South-facing land** | Light arrives from everywhere across a season | No sun-facing vineyards, no favoured slopes. Terracing is about drainage alone |

### What remains

**The moons rise and set.** Each dips below the horizon and climbs back over it on its own reliable schedule, each with its own soft glow. They are the only large moving objects in the sky that behave.

**The scattered lights hold station.** When the sun has faded, the dark sky carries a scatter of small steady lights, called a dozen different things depending on who raised you. What they are is not known and not currently recorded. What matters is that **they do not move relative to one another**, and they are consistent enough to steer by. *(Naming deferred — see Open Questions.)*

So: **the moons give you time, and the lights give you direction.** The sun gives you neither. It gives you warmth and the ability to see your hands, and it gives them back on no schedule anyone can defend.

### Why this makes the coast dangerous

Direction on Austhal requires a clear sky. Overcast means **lost** — not delayed, lost. On a storm-lashed coast fringed by marsh fog, that is not an edge case; it is the ordinary condition of half the year.

This is the structural reason that:
- **Marsh-guides are a profession** rather than a convenience, and why hiring one at Fenmouth is legally mandated rather than merely sensible
- **The [[Silted Marshes]] kill people who are not incompetent.** A shifting channel network with no directional reference under cloud is a maze that rearranges itself
- **[[Captain Haren Twarde]] began as a conscripted navigator** and that was an elite technical post, not a menial one. Reading the fixed lights through broken cloud is a rare and valuable skill, and it is why she could take a ship
- **Master Cartographer Vaelen's** work is genuinely hard. He is mapping a world with no solar reference, in which every bearing on every chart traces back to lights nobody can explain
- **The Tidespoken tide-tables** are trusted over the Council's civil roll by every harbour pilot in the city. The clergy read the moons. The clerks read a calendar

### The theological weight

The sun does not rise. **It comes back.**

To every faith on the coast, and to a great many people with no faith at all, this is the plain daily fact of existence: the light went out, and it was under no obligation to return, and it did. Nobody has ever been owed a morning. Fifty-eight years of Accord, a hundred generations of settlement, and an entire dead pantheon rotting in the sky — and the reprieve has arrived every single time so far.

That is the setting's whole thesis, written into its physics. It is also why the **Low Moons** frighten people at a level below argument. For nine to twelve nights, the only things in the sky that have ever kept their promises are visibly behaving wrong.

## 🌑 The Problem the Moons Solve

The Great Disk is flat. It has no axial tilt, no orbit, and no hemispheres. **There is no mechanism for seasons.** This is a genuine hole, and it has exactly one answer available that doesn't require new cosmology:

**The moons cause the seasons.** They are not lights. They are masses of dead god-flesh, and when they ride low and close they drag — on tide, on weather, on the thin unanchored energy still bleeding out of the shards buried in the Slack Water. Winter is not a tilt. Winter is **weight**.

This is why every season on the coast is named for moons and not for temperature, why sailors and alchemists share a reckoning that farmers only borrow, and why the Low Moons are dangerous rather than merely dark. It is the same phenomenon at its extreme.

## 🌘 The Three Moons

Sailors name them for what they look like. The temples name them for what they think died up there. Which name a person uses tells you where they were raised — the same tell the [[Cosmology - The Great Fracture|Tideways]] labels give.

| Common name | Temple name | Cycle | Appearance | Associated with |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Pale Sister** | *Nyssaria* | 28 days | Silver-white, fast, unremarkable | The civil turn. Sets the month. Everyone's moon |
| **The Drowned Lamp** | *Ossuel* | 40 days | Dim, green-tinged, low on the horizon | Tides, brine, the Tidespoken. Sailors' moon |
| **The Slow Wound** | *Cassivar* | 105 days | Red-brown, barely seems to move | Alchemy, blight, the Low Moons. Watched, not loved |

**The Pale Sister governs the calendar** because she is the only one regular enough to build a civil year on. This is a practical decision made by clerks, not a theological one, and the Tidespoken have never fully forgiven it.

## 📅 The Civil Year

**13 turns × 28 days = 364 days, + The Unwritten Day = 365.**

A **turn** is one full cycle of the Pale Sister. Turns are grouped into four seasons of unequal length, because the moons do not care about symmetry.

| Season | Turns | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **The Winter Moons** | Hollow · Deepcold · Sleetbreak | Passes close. The Coastal Meridian gets dangerous. Rural coast goes inward |
| **The Opening** | First Keel · Gullmarch · Greenrot | The spring trade season. Port Nevarellon's population doubles |
| **The High Turns** | Longlight · Saltdraw · Highsun · Dogtide | Salt-pans, fishing, campaigning weather. The Duchies posture in these turns |
| **The Draw-Down** | Sheafturn · Lastwater · Ashfall | Harvest, cistern-measuring, debt-reckoning. The year's accounts close |
| **⟡ The Unwritten Day** | — | Falls between Ashfall and Hollow. Belongs to no turn |

Several turns take their name from the festival that defines them — *First Keel*, *Saltdraw*, *Lastwater* are all in `Reference - Name Tables` Table 3. This is deliberate and historically normal: people name the month after the thing they do in it.

## ⟡ The Unwritten Day

**The 365th day sits outside the turns. Legally, it is not a date.**

This began as a rounding error and became the single most contested day in Port Nevarellon. Because it belongs to no turn, an instrument that specifies a turn cannot fall due on it. In practice:

- **Vagrancy Tolls** have no legal basis for collection — the Edict specifies collection days by turn
- **Golden Writs** cannot lapse on it, and cannot be issued on it
- **Debts cannot be called**, and the debt-prisons take no new admissions
- **A Nullity Sitting cannot be convened** — the [[Religion - The pagan Pantheon and the Faith Domains|Cult of the Zenith]] holds that a day outside the reckoning cannot host a judgment. This is doctrinally awkward for them and they do not enjoy discussing it

The [[Council of Five|Council of Five]] has attempted to absorb the day into Hollow or Ashfall four times in fifty-eight years. Each attempt failed, twice loudly. **The Toll Amnesty** *(Table 3, #3)* is what the Council calls its annual defeat — a mercy announced from the steps, granted with great ceremony, and legally unavoidable.

For one day a year, the Un-Landed of Port Nevarellon can walk into the High Quarter without paying to do it. Most don't. But it is theirs, and everyone knows it.

## 🔴 The Low Moons

All three moons ride low together when their cycles align: **every 840 days — exactly 30 turns, a little over two years.**

`LCM(28, 40, 105) = 840`

The alignment holds for **nine to twelve nights**, during which the effects already in canon apply: the war-bred instincts of the monstrous races stir, Undertow-touched alchemical stock turns unstable, and **Brine-Fire** becomes something nobody sane keeps indoors. *(Needs crunch: hazard modifiers, encounter shifts, alchemical failure rates.)*

**The important structural property: 30 turns does not divide into 13.** The Low Moons advance four turns through the calendar every cycle. They walk the year.

| Cycle falls in | Consequence |
|---|---|
| The Winter Moons | Survivable. Everyone is already indoors, the passes are already shut |
| The Opening | **Economic catastrophe.** The trade season cannot open; a year's wages evaporate |
| The High Turns | Campaigning season. The Duchies cannot move levies. Border disputes freeze |
| The Draw-Down | Harvest lost or rushed. Debt-reckoning arrives with nothing to pay it |

A generation can grow up having only known winter Low Moons and be genuinely unprepared for one that lands in Gullmarch. The last Opening-cycle Low Moons was **43 A.A.** Anyone under about thirty has never worked through one.

## 🔑 Who Owns the Calendar

**This is a Layer 1 chokepoint and it is currently unassigned.** Per [[Framework - The Three Layers|the Three Layers]], whoever computes the reckoning decides when every instrument in the city falls due.

| Body | What they compute | Their claim |
|---|---|---|
| **[[The High Alchemist Guild\|The High Alchemist Guild]]** | The Low Moons tables, reckoned in **days** | They must, for the Brine-Glow and Brine-Fire stock. The tables are their property and they sell access |
| **The [[Council of Five\|Council of Five]]** | The civil year, reckoned in **turns** | Contracts, tolls, writs, tariff dates. The Council's clerks publish the turn-roll |
| **The Cult of the Zenith** | Nothing — and resents it | Holds that a reckoning is a legal instrument and therefore theirs. Has never been able to make that stick |
| **The Tidespoken Clergy** | The Drowned Lamp's tide-tables | Dispute the Pale Sister's primacy outright. Every harbour pilot uses Tidespoken tables anyway |

**The five fields, applied to the calendar itself:**
- **Chokepoint:** The day-count. Not grain, not water — the shared answer to *when*.
- **Enforcement:** Scarcity and habit. The Guild's tables are expensive; the Council's turn-roll is free and therefore universal.
- **Geographic Anchor:** The Guild's observatory and instrument room; the Council's clerk-house in the Trade Plazas.
- **Bypass:** Tidespoken tide-tables, and the pilots who trust them over the civil roll.
- **Cost & Mote:** The Guild sells the only reliable warning of the Low Moons, which means the poor get warned last — *and* the Tidespoken give theirs away free at the Sea-Wall, which is the only reason the Docks ever get warned at all.

**The day/turn mismatch is a live exploit.** The Guild reckons in days, the Council in turns, and the Unwritten Day sits between them. A sufficiently well-paid advocate can and does argue that an instrument fell due on a date that, by one reckoning, did not occur. This is not a bug to be fixed. It is where a certain kind of adventure starts.

## 🗓️ Dating & The Epoch

The King-Ban makes regnal dating illegal. The coast therefore counts from the only event everyone was present for:

**A.A. — After Accord.** The current year is **58 A.A.**

| Reckoning | Used by | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **A.A.** | Port Nevarellon, the Concord Road, all legal instruments | The merchant epoch. Universal in writing |
| **House years** | The four surviving Duchies, informally | Counted from each house's founding. Never appears on paper, constantly appears in speech. A De Vonce steward saying "the year of the house" in front of a Council factor is making a point |
| **Twelgorn reckoning** | The southern kingdom | Counts from its own dynasty, centuries deeper. Twelgorn documents dated by their own kings are *legally unusable* on the coast, which is a diplomatic problem nobody has solved |

**Fixed points on the A.A. scale:**

| Year | Event | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **0 A.A.** | The last King slain; the Ducal Accord signed; the 99-Year Contract executed | `History - The Broken Crown of Austhal` |
| **~8 A.A.** | Corvus Spire falls. The Ash-Blight begins | Derived — `The Five Duchies` states "fifty years later" for the refugee crisis |
| **43 A.A.** | Last Low Moons to fall in The Opening | Derived from the 840-day cycle |
| **58 A.A.** | **Present day.** 41 years remain on the 99-Year Contract | `History` — "nearly six decades on" |

⚠️ **The 8 A.A. Corvus date is derived, not stated.** It reconciles "fifty years" (Corvus) against "nearly six decades" (the exiles in Twelgorn) — two different events, not a contradiction. Confirm before it goes in the canon tracker.

**41 years left on the Contract** is worth sitting with. The Contract Bell *(Table 3, #7)* rings once per remaining year. Everyone in Port Nevarellon has heard that count get shorter their whole life, and nobody has any idea what happens at zero.
