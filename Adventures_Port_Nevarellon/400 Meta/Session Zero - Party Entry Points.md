# Session Zero: Party Entry Points
#meta/session-zero #status/draft

> "Nobody on this coast travels with strangers by choice. You travel with whoever else said yes." — a Concord Road carter

> **GM reference.** Six ways to start a party, for a session zero. Each one is a pitch the players can read, written in-world: it is what their characters know, and they may be wrong. Under each pitch:
> - what holds the party together past the first night;
> - one hook for each of the seven roster characters (The Meat);
> - an **open slot**, so a player can build their own character into it;
> - an opening scene;
> - a **GM-facing** callout with the truth, and what the entry point commits to canon.
>
> Use any four to six of the roster. Every entry point runs toward the same clock: **the Low Moons fall in the Opening of 59 A.A.**, the first in the trade season since 43 A.A. Nobody under about thirty has worked through one (Framework - The Coastal Reckoning). Each one also leans on the Starting Purse. Every character came by eighty silver somehow, and spent it to get out of the life they had.

## 1 · The Boot-Change
**Port Nevarellon, the Foundry Slips. Sleetbreak, 58 A.A.**

> *Pitch.* Once a year, in the last cold month before the Opening, the Union stands in the Foundry Slips and hands out the year: a token, a gang, and the front of the line at every dawn token-call. You were there. You were passed over, again, or you never had a chance. When the crowd thinned, a Guild clerk with ink on his cuffs walked along the ones who were left and said he needed hands that weren't on anyone's books. The Low Moons are coming in the Opening, and every pot of Brine-Fire in the city has to be sealed and logged before then. The Union won't touch it in a Low Moons year. The Guild pays by the day, in coin, and asks no questions about where you sleep.

- **Why together:** the same clerk, the same pay-chit, and the same Powder Hulk.
- **Why stay:** the work runs until the Shuttering, and the first night's tally doesn't match the Guild's own ledger.

| Character | Why they're in the line |
|---|---|
| **Tass** | Passed over again. She owes Maeve eighty silver, and day wages won't pay it |
| **Dilly** | The Guild pays in coin, and coin buys down Wenna's bond. The Powder Hulk is also the one hull in the harbour she's never been inside |
| **Skell** | Work where nobody asks for papers. He has never signed one |
| **Elrid** | Brine-Fire burns people. The Guild wants someone who can keep a burnt man alive until the surgeon comes |
| **Nirael** | She can read a burn. The clerk saw the scorch on her hands and stopped walking |
| **Durga** | Down from the passes for the winter, with no keep paying. A hunter knows how to carry something that wants to kill her |
| **Iolo** | New in the city, his salt-pan bond bought out, and a caller keeps a gang in time. The Guild has a gang |
| **Open slot** | You're here because (pick one): you're on a Union blacklist · you need coin before the Opening and can't wait for the season · someone in the Guild owes you a favour, and this is how they paid it |

**Opening scene:** the dawn of the Boot-Change. Rain, the Union boss's voice, a name that isn't yours, then the clerk.

> **GM-facing.**
> - **The truth:** the Guild's Brine-Fire count is short before anyone has sealed a pot. Maeve has been shading the Iron-Anchor's manifests to stall Silas Bane, and Silas has been taking the difference from the Guild's stock ([[Maeve the Scribe]]; [[Silas Bane]]). The party are hired to seal what is left. They will be the first to notice what is missing, and the obvious people to blame.
> - **Where it leads:** Silas's plot against the Brine-Glow Lanterns, the Shuttering, and the Opening Low Moons in the city.
> - **New canon:** the **Boot-Change falls in Sleetbreak**, the last month of the Winter Moons, so the year's gangs are set before the trade season opens. **Silas Bane has been taking Brine-Fire from Guild stock** under cover of Maeve's shaded manifests.
> - **Mote:** the Tongues' kitchens on the lower piers feed the Shuttering crews for free, as they fed Tass's family.

## 2 · The Paper
**Saltmere and Brinewick, Duchy of Valerius. Saltdraw, 58 A.A.**

> *Pitch.* Everyone you owe has sold you. Your loans, your bonds, the debts on people you love were bought up in one season by the Valerius bond-houses at Saltmere, and the house's letter reached you the same week. The letter offers a clean book, every copper struck off, for ten days' work at the Brinewick Salt-Draw. There will be a crowd, a lot-bag, a song, and people who would rather the song were not sung. Keep the singer alive and the crowd in its place until the Draw is done. The galley to Saltmere is paid for. Refuse, and the paper goes to whoever bids next.

- **Why together:** one buyer, one letter, and one debt each that only the job can clear.
- **Why stay:** the man who bought the paper never says who he speaks for, and the debts are only struck when the Table sits.

| Character | What was bought |
|---|---|
| **Tass** | Her eighty silver, bought out of Maeve's ledger. Lassen is named as surety |
| **Dilly** | Wenna's Debt-Bond, and with it the hulks |
| **Skell** | No paper: he never signed. So the house bought the next best thing, a Retriever's warrant with his marks described on it |
| **Elrid** | Barleywick's tenancy debt to the Millhaven factors, and his name on it as a runaway |
| **Nirael** | The burning rights the three Ashcopse families lost. The house will give them back if she works |
| **Durga** | Two winters' keep-fees she drew in advance and spent. The bond-house bought the debt |
| **Iolo** | Nothing. He is the singer. The man in the good coat paid him in advance, and this is the rest of the plan |
| **Open slot** | Someone bought: a debt you ran from · a family member's bond · your guild dues, if you ever had a guild |

**Opening scene:** the Saltmere quay at dawn, the galley's gangplank, and the man in the good coat counting heads against a list.

> **GM-facing.**
> - **The truth:** the buyer is Gratian's faction. Brinewick singing the Junia verse at Salt-Draw costs Junia her yield in the week the Table sits, and opens the move to remove her ([[Valerius Family]]). Junia's people know a song is coming. So does Tallis Gorrius, who would rather nothing interrupted the rakes.
> - **Iolo's secret:** **Thala Skerry** is still raking, and she is in the crowd.
> - **Where it leads:** the Table, the Hordeus bonds and Aerthos, and Valerius credit reaching into every other duchy.
> - **New canon:** none. The bond-houses' habit of buying debt fits their place in the Guilds framework.
> - **Mote:** the Crock. One worker walks free at every Salt-Draw, and this year the whole square knows a song about it.

## 3 · The Ford
**Ashcopse to Bittern's Ford, Duchy of De Vonce. Greenrot, 58 A.A.**

> *Pitch.* A boy in Ashcopse woke with his fingers longer than they should be, hooked, and the palms gone to pad. His mother hid him in the charcoal-stack for three days. On the fourth, word came up from Hollowforge that Tidespoken Tongues were on the Road asking about Ashcopse, and the burners want it dealt with before they arrive. There is one place in De Vonce where no village kills its changed: north over the Adderwater, past the castellan at Bittern's Ford, into the Scar. Somebody has to walk him there. You're who was standing nearby.

- **Why together:** one boy, one road north, and everyone behind you.
- **Why stay:** the ford is three days out, the Tongues ride, and a castellan who lets a changed boy through needs to see that the people bringing him are worth her trouble.

| Character | Why they're walking north |
|---|---|
| **Tass** | Hired out of the city with a carter's convoy that turned back at Hollowforge. She has never seen a Low Moons, and she's never seen this either |
| **Dilly** | The boy's mother paid her in charcoal-cutter's silver to get him out. Dilly knows what hiding a body from the Watch looks like |
| **Skell** | Somebody bled for him once and got him north. Nobody else will do it for this boy |
| **Elrid** | He laid hands on the boy, and the laying-on found nothing to take |
| **Nirael** | **The boy is from one of the three families she informed on.** They lost their burning rights because of her, and now they are about to lose him |
| **Durga** | She knows what comes down off a mountain and what the keeps pay to see it dead. She knows the boy is neither |
| **Iolo** | He has walked out of a life once. He knows the songs that keep a frightened child walking |
| **Open slot** | You're here because: you're kin to the boy · you were changed once yourself and hid it · you owe the Scar-Holders at the Quiet Steading, and this pays it |

**Opening scene:** the charcoal-stack at night. The turf is warm, the boy is hiding, and his mother has a torch in one hand and a knife in the other, and hasn't decided which she'll use.

> **GM-facing.**
> - **The truth:** the boy's change was **Seeped** from Longbarrow grain: Hook-Hands, a gift on the table and a death sentence in De Vonce (Iron Core, *Mutation*). The Tidespoken have taken the hunt up the Road ([[The Tidespoken Clergy]]).
> - **The ford:** Senna Cade lets the changed through and does not look closely ([[Castellan Senna Cade]]). Eldrick's captains on the test-ground in the upper Sisters would rather have him as a test.
> - **Where it leads:** the Scar-Holders, the Quiet Steading, Eldrick's weapons and the war-bone.
> - **New canon:** the boy (name him when play needs it) is the son of one of the three families who lost their burning rights. **Placed:** Ashcopse, in the Iron Wood.
> - **Mote:** the Quiet Steading takes him in, and brings the first of its beans to Senna Cade, as it always has.

## 4 · The Unchaining
**Divtown, the Silted Marshes. Any season.**

> *Pitch.* Once a year Divtown turns out for the Unchaining, to mark a slave one full year free. You came for it, or you came to hide in a town where nobody asks for papers. This year a Retriever crew poled in the same week, with a Twelgorn warrant nobody here has to honour and enough hired blades that nobody wants to test it. They are asking after one name. Before the rite is over, someone you would rather not lose will be on their list.

- **Why together:** a town of the unfree, a crew hunting one of you, and a guide who will only take you out together.
- **Why stay:** the marsh doesn't let small groups out alive, and the Retrievers aren't leaving.

| Character | Why they're in Divtown |
|---|---|
| **Tass** | Iron-Anchor business: Maeve wants a ledger carried back from Kelf Thorne's fences, and the debt makes Tass the carrier |
| **Dilly** | One of the Retrievers' hired blades is a man she informed on for the Blue-Cloaks. He doesn't know it yet |
| **Skell** | The Unchaining is the one night a year the survivors of his company meet. **This year a sixth face is at the fire**, one he buried in his mind years ago |
| **Elrid** | The steward's man who came looking for him at the House has sold his warrants on. One of them has come south |
| **Nirael** | One of the dead Arcanist's letters is addressed to Divtown |
| **Durga** | Iron-Burl trade brought her down from the passes for a winter of felling. A Long-Snare goes where the work is |
| **Iolo** | The free-salt men ride with the Crooked Coin, and the Coin keeps a table in Divtown |
| **Open slot** | You're here because: you were owned once · you buy and sell what Divtown fences · you are hiding from a warrant of your own |

**Opening scene:** the Unchaining. Rope bridges full of people, a name read out, chains thrown into the lagoon, and a Retriever in the crowd who isn't cheering.

> **GM-facing.**
> - **The truth:** the sixth face is bait. The Retrievers kept one of Skell's company alive to bring the rest in, and the warrant they show is for Skell.
> - **Lord Kelf Thorne** will sell the party or shelter them, whichever costs him less ([[Divtown]]). Morgran will take them out through the Grey Veins if they carry the dead with them.
> - **Where it leads:** the Twelgorn Kingdom, the Retrievers and the Iron-Burl trade.
> - **New canon:** the sixth survivor (name them from Register C when needed). **Placed:** Divtown, at the Unchaining.
> - **Mote:** the Unchaining itself. The whole town turns out to see someone stay free for one more year.

## 5 · The Letters
**From Ashcopse, down the Concord Road and along the coast. Any season.**

> *Pitch.* A charcoal-burner from De Vonce is carrying a dead man's book and a bundle of letters folded into its back board, each to someone different. She has delivered two. Each recipient opened theirs, went very quiet, and paid her to leave. She is hiring, or asking: the next letter is addressed somewhere she cannot walk into alone.

- **Why together:** each letter opens a door that needs one of you to walk through it.
- **Why stay:** the letters are all from one dead man, the people opening them are frightened, and the captains who hired him have started asking questions.

| Character | Where their letter is going |
|---|---|
| **Tass** | To a counting-house clerk in the Basin she has carried day-tokens for. She knows the way in |
| **Dilly** | To Lidda Shoon. Dilly's fence is in a dead Arcanist's correspondence, which nobody knew |
| **Skell** | To a Fenmouth guide. The marsh is the only way to get there, and he's the only one who knows it |
| **Elrid** | To the House of Mercy, addressed to Mother Marra |
| **Nirael** | She carries them. The Grimoire is hers now, and so is whatever the letters start |
| **Durga** | To a Fang-mother at Sleetfall, up the passes |
| **Iolo** | To a Valerius bond-house at Saltmere. He knows the Table's people by their coats |
| **Open slot** | One letter is addressed to you, or to someone you used to be |

**Opening scene:** Nirael at a table, the book open, two letters already gone, and five still folded in the back board.

> **GM-facing.**
> - **The truth:** the letters are the dead Arcanist's correspondence (Lore - Arcana: *a correspondence*). Together they say who taught him to make Eldrick's weapons and who else knows how ([[Duchy of De Vonce]], *Who taught him?*).
> - **Where it leads:** Eldrick's weapons, the Guild's moon-tables, and the essence the Arcanist bought from Marrowmill.
> - **New canon, my recommendation:** the five remaining letters go to **Mother Marra** (the House of Mercy), **Lidda Shoon**, **a Fang-mother at Sleetfall**, **a Valerius bond-house at Saltmere** (where the money came from) and **someone in Divtown**. Each table uses only the letters its party can carry. The two already delivered stay open.
> - **Mote:** a letter that is only a letter. One recipient is a widow, and the dead man was her son.

## 6 · The Match
**Castle Iron-Spire to Granite Spire, by the Concord Road. Late Dogtide, 58 A.A.**

> *Pitch.* The Duke of De Vonce has a letter for the Duke of Stonereach and does not want it carried by anyone in his colours. The pay is good and the instructions are simple: take the Concord Road through the passes to Granite Spire, six to eight days, and put it into Duke Brennus's hand before the Winter Moons close the Road. Speak to nobody about it at Iron-Spire. Speak to nobody about it in Stonereach until you are in the Toll Hall. If the seal is broken, do not come back.

- **Why together:** a sealed letter, a closing road, and a ducal promise that only holds if it arrives.
- **Why stay:** someone at Iron-Spire knows the letter left, and the passes don't wait.

| Character | Why they took the letter |
|---|---|
| **Tass** | It's paid work beyond the walls, exactly what she borrowed eighty silver to kit out for |
| **Dilly** | A ducal seal is the best lock she has ever been asked not to open |
| **Skell** | The Duke's steward offered him the work without asking for papers. He wants to know why |
| **Elrid** | The passes kill travellers in Hollow. A priest who can close a wound is worth his wage |
| **Nirael** | Eldrick's captains hired her once, and she wants to be far from them. This is far |
| **Durga** | **The road ends at Granite Spire**, and the Keystone crews' quarters are under it |
| **Iolo** | He wants out of the city before Salt-Draw, and a ducal courier needs a voice that can talk past a toll-clerk |
| **Open slot** | You're here because: the Duke's steward chose you for being nobody · you know the passes · you need to be in Stonereach before the snow for reasons of your own |

**Opening scene:** a side gate at Castle Iron-Spire after dark. A steward hands over a letter wrapped in oilcloth, and a lamp goes out in a high window as you leave.

> **GM-facing.**
> - **The truth:** the letter presses the Stonereach match: Sheandri for Cato Scutarius. It is sweetened, and it must arrive before the Low Moons. Tythius has told no one why ([[Duchy of De Vonce]]). The lamp in the window is **Eldrick's**: he knows a letter went, and not what it says.
> - **Where it leads:**
>   - the Long Graft and Ubaraz's tolls;
>   - **Sleetfall and the Unburied** ([[Duchy of Stonereach]]; Playtest A5), a natural detour;
>   - Sheandri's refusal;
>   - Aerthos, which reached Stonereach first.
> - **New canon:** none beyond the courier party; the letter's exact terms are the GM's.
> - **Mote:** storm-right. When the storm-lamp burns on Granite Spire, the western gate opens to anyone, and the Keystone crews come up the stair to carry the half-frozen in.
