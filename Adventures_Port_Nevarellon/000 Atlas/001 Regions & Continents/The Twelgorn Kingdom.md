# Polity: The Twelgorn Kingdom (Tuwal Ghorun)
#location/region #faction/state #status/draft

> "Down south they'll tell you the King is blessed. I carried water to one. There's a god under that city, and it doesn't bless anybody. It keeps what it's owed." — Tahra Beyr

---

## 🏷️ Names
- **Tuwal Ghorun** is the name of the god, and the realm is named for it. Its people, its rulers and its documents all use that name.
- **Twelgorn** is the Whispering Coast's word, worn down from *Tuwal Ghorun*. No coastal register lists the true name. A southern instrument therefore names a polity the Register cannot match to anything, and it can be neither enforced nor condemned on the coast. This is the **identity void** — see [[History - The Broken Crown of Austhal]].
- **The ruler's title is *the Al Ghorun*** — "of Ghorun" — and it carries no gender. The coast translates it as **King**, so the Whispering Coast calls this a kingdom and calls its ruler a King whoever sits the seat.
- **Dating.** Tuwal Ghorun counts its years from **the Binding**, centuries before the Whispering Coast was settled. Documents dated in years of the Binding are legally unusable on the coast (see [[Framework - The Coastal Reckoning]]).

## 🗺️ Geography & Scope
The realm runs south from the far edge of the [[Silted Marshes]] in five bands:

1. **The marsh fringe.** Garrison-forts on the southern edge of the marshes, supplied by seasonal payroll convoys. This is Retriever country, and the northern limit of Tuwal Ghorun's reach.
2. **The coast.** Subject port-cities on the far southern coast, older than the realm's conquest of them. They are mercantile and literate, and their people use the Southern (Register C) naming. The navy is built and crewed here.
3. **The steppe.** Arid grass plains, horse-country, the fiefs of the steppe-lords, and **the capital**, raised over the site of the Binding.
4. **The native belt.** Beyond the capital, where the plains grow harsher before the mountains, live the region's **native peoples**. They are older than the realm and never submitted to it. They survive between Tuwal Ghorun's slave-raids and the mountain folk's hunting parties.
5. **The plateau ranges.** Flat-topped mountain ranges held by **giants and ogres**. They are war-bred monstrous peoples, and they eat the dead — their own and others'. This is a practice born of a starving plateau and old war-rites, not a nature. Tuwal Ghorun tells the story as if it were nature, because that story justifies enslaving anyone of monstrous descent (see [[Maccorrack]]).

- **Terrain & climate:** a marsh-fringe of silt and reed rising onto dry steppe; long sightlines, hard winters, little wood. The coast is the only place with timber, which is why the realm buys **Iron-Burl** through shell brokers.
- **Travel time to Port Nevarellon:** *(to be set)* — at least the full 4–7 day marsh crossing to reach the fringe forts, and weeks overland to the capital.

## 👑 Rule & Succession
- **The Blessing.** Anyone may claim the seat by presenting themselves at the Binding and asking Tuwal Ghorun for the Blessing. Most who ask die of it. The one who survives is **Reach-touched** — the High Reach's counterpart to Morgran's Undertow-touched change — and gains great might and vigour. The survivor becomes the Al Ghorun.
- **The cost.** The vigour burns hot. The blessed rule hard and do not rule long, so claimants are never scarce and the fiefs are never quite settled.
- **The Gathering.** The steppe-lords convene to witness each claim. They cannot choose the ruler — only the god does that — but they decide whose claim is heard first, which is its own kind of power.
- **The fiefs.** The realm is a loose web of fiefdoms, all of which bow to the Al Ghorun. Day to day, disputes go to the next more powerful lord, and so on up the chain; the Al Ghorun is the last appeal, and rarely troubled. This is the "aristocratic officer corps" the coast meets aboard Tuwal Ghorun's warships.
- **The current ruler:** [[Zafira Al Munn]] *(no file yet)*. She was once a skilled slave — a factor who bought Iron-Burl for her owners through three shell brokers — and she claimed the seat and survived the Blessing. She has kept slavery exactly as she found it.

## 🕯️ Tuwal Ghorun, the Bound God
Religion in Tuwal Ghorun is monotheistic, and its god is real.

- **What it is.** Not a god in the old sense. It is the largest *intact* remnant of a Creator to survive the Deicide: a Reach-aligned god-essence that should have risen to the High Reach and instead lies held in the Slack Water beneath the capital. The coast's cults teach that there are no living gods answering prayers (see [[Religion - The Pagan Pantheon and the Faith Domains]]). On the coast, that is true. Tuwal Ghorun is the exception, and almost no one on the coast knows it.
- **The Binding.** The realm's founders bound it using the same stolen craft the mortals turned on their Creators. The Binding is the state's real foundation and the start of its calendar. Its keepers are a priesthood the coast calls **the Binders** *(own name to be set)*.
- **What it gives.** The Blessing. What the god takes in return is not recorded anywhere the coast can read.
- **The omen.** For fifty-eight years the god has told the realm to wait for a sign before moving north: **the Low Moons falling in the Opening**, the next of which is due around **59 A.A.** (see [[Framework - The Coastal Reckoning]]). The Opening is the coast's worst season to be struck — the trade season fails and the Duchies are caught with their levies idle.

> **GM-facing.** Tuwal Ghorun cannot lie, but it chooses what it says. The omen is true and incomplete. The Low Moons destabilise anything touched by god-essence, and a Binding is exactly that. The invasion the realm thinks is prophecy is the god's escape. Its fifty-eight years of patience were never the realm's restraint.

## ⛓️ Slavery
Slaves are the realm's principal trade good and its working body.
- **Uses.** Art, music, scholarship and teaching, household service, the mines, the oars and rigging of the navy, and the ranks of the army.
- **The ink.** Ownership is **tattooed**. Each sale adds an owner's mark, and a slave can carry a patchwork of owners across a whole life — a ledger written on the skin. Tattoos can be covered.
- **The brand.** **Brands** are the realm's own mark. They go on galley and mine slaves held as property of the realm, and on recaptured runaways. Brands cannot be covered. [[Tahra Beyr]]'s back is branded: her escape north was her second.
- **Skill buys comfort.** A slave whose skill their owner values can live well — better than many free steppe herders. Manumission exists and is recorded (Name Tables: Ghurad Oss carries papers he can't read).
- **Sources.** Raids on the native belt, captured monstrous-descent people, war captives, and debtors from the port-cities.
- **Reach north.** Escapees run for the marshes; the **Retrievers** follow them. On the coast a Retriever's warrant names a polity no register lists, so it has no force — which is why Retrievers work in the marsh, where no law reaches either way.

## 💰 Economy & Trade
- **An overland trade empire.** Caravans cross the steppe, and silks, spices and bullion reach the southern sea through the port-cities. On the coast these goods are simply "Twelgorn silks" and "Twelgorn gold".
- **Treasure galleons sail north**, and seasonal payroll convoys supply the marsh-fringe garrisons — prey for [[Captain Haren Twarde]].
- **Trade through the void.** [[Palla Vantry]]'s houses move southern goods through the identity void, where they can be neither taxed nor condemned.
- **What the realm lacks:** timber and powder. It buys Iron-Burl through brokers, because what little timber it has rots at sea. It buys powder from [[The Guild of Alchemists|the Guild of Alchemists]]' chapter in the southern port-cities — blasting powder for the slave-mines, and charges for the steppe-lords' pistols.

---

## ⚔️ Military
- **The steppe host.** Horse-lords and their riders, organised by fief — the realm's true strength, and useless at sea.
- **The navy.** Deep-draft, iron-clad "floating fortresses", built by slave shipwrights in the port-cities, crewed by slaves and conscripts, and commanded by steppe-lords who never learned the sea. Conscripted navigators like Haren were the only real seamen aboard. Heavy hulls that run aground on the silt-banks are the navy's standing weakness, and Haren built her career on it.
- **The Retrievers** project the realm's authority into the marshes.

## 👤 The Exiled Royal Line
- **The guests.** Since 0 A.A. the surviving children of the last King of the Whispering Coast and their descendants have lived in Tuwal Ghorun as "guests" of the Al Ghorun. The portable wealth they fled with is long spent. They are **poor dependants**, kept, housed and watched. Their claim to the coast passes from ruler to ruler like anything else in the treasury.
- **The Unaging Heir** *(name to be set)*. One of the original children who fled at 0 A.A. was given a *partial* Blessing. It stopped their ageing and gave them nothing else. Fifty-eight years on, they look exactly as they did the night they fled. They are recognisable, and they are the reason Tuwal Ghorun has kept the line at all.
- **The loophole.** The Ducal Accord's ultimate law says no *mortal* may hold or claim the moniker of King. The Heir's claim is that they are no longer mortal. Only one court on the coast could rule on that, and the [[The Cult of the Zenith|Cult of the Zenith]]'s Court of Nullity can void a claim but never confirm one. The question of whether the Heir is bound by the King-Ban has no forum that can answer it yes.
- **Who could recognise the Heir on sight:** [[Palla Vantry]], who was in the room when the last King died, and [[Tythius De Vonce]], who witnessed his execution.
- **The invasion's shape.** A move north would be a war of conquest wearing a legitimacy costume — and the legitimacy would arrive in person, unaged.

## 🧱 Layer & the Five Fields
Tuwal Ghorun sits **outside the Layers**: a foreign power pressing on Layer 1 from the south, with the Retrievers as its Layer 2 presence in the marshes.
- **Chokepoint:** the Binding — the bound god beneath the capital and the Blessing it grants.
- **Enforcement:** violence (the steppe host, the navy) and faith (the Blessing makes the ruler), with ownership written in ink.
- **Geographic anchor:** the Binding site beneath the capital.
- **Bypass:** the Low Moons loosen the Binding; runaways reach the marshes; the identity void cuts the realm's paper off at the coast.
- **Cost & mote:** the cost is everyone the realm owns. The mote is that the seat is open to anyone who survives the Blessing — a slave can become the Al Ghorun, and one did. The native belt shelters runaways; skilled slaves teach the children of the lords who own them; and every year Divtown turns out for the Unchaining.
