# Ledger House Job — Playtest Log (Simulated Run)

**Party:** Corvin Ashgrave (Bravo/Duelist), Wren Ashcombe (Halfling thief), Helga Stonewright (Dwarf Law priest). Run as a fully-diced simulation (genuine RNG, no survival bias) rather than a live table session — filed against the checklist in the original playtest doc, plus additional seams that surfaced along the way.

## Grave-Warden / Faith math

- **Did a PC cast Wrathful Light or Rime-Fang's Bite? Outcome:** No. **This roster cannot exercise this test at all** — Helga is Law Domain (no offensive Miracle in her list), and nobody else has Faith investment. The flat-2-Impact-can-never-cross-WT4 question needs a Mercy/Healing or otherwise offense-capable Faith build in the party; recommend re-running this specific scene with Perpetua Vane or an equivalent instead of Helga.
- **Did Fear Inducing land? What ruling did you improvise?** Yes, on both Wren and Helga at the top of the fight. Discovered a **contradiction between the playtest doc and Iron Core**: the playtest doc claims Fear "has no written way to shake it off," but Iron Core's own Conditions list already states Fear clears "by taking the Regroup action when out of sight or has cover from the object/being causing the fear." That fix may already be in place and the playtest flag is simply stale — worth confirming. Separately, we found the clearance route is close to _unusable_ in this fight's actual geometry: a single-room strongroom against one melee guardian gives no cover to break line of sight, so even with the rule on the books, players defaulted to just re-rolling Resolve each activation instead of spending a full turn on Regroup. Also improvised: Fear auto-clears when its source is destroyed mid-condition (no RAW answer either way).

## The Watch

- **Evade, fight, or caught? At which stage?** Evaded. The Grave-Warden fight (loud, per the explicit trigger) pushed the Watch straight to Stage 1 (Alerted) the moment it ended. One opposed Stealth check at Difficult (-2) got the party past the only checkpoint we rolled for (Margin +1 — closer than it looked). Never escalated to Stage 2 Pursuit.
- **Did Suppressed create real pressure, or fizzle?** Never tested — the Watch never got close enough to loose crossbows. Worth a dedicated run.
- **Did the Cunning Leader/Fodder-Threat question come up?** No — no Watch combat occurred, so Patrolmen never got the chance to spend (or fail to spend) Threat. Still an open rules question.

## The Sluice Vault

- **Did Drowned feel oppressive, or clear trivially?** Never really tested as _designed_ — the dice pushed past Drowned entirely. Wren's Passive Notice (7, untrained) failed the Aware/Unaware check, and because **Unaware targets get no defense roll of any kind against a Hazard Roll**, one bad trap roll (14 vs her Wound Threshold 4) skipped Drowned's slow-burn tension and went straight to **Instant Incapacitation** (Impact ≥ 3× Threshold). That's a real finding on its own: a squishy Unaware character facing a Hazard Roll has zero agency in the outcome — not even a bad roll to make. Worth deciding if Unaware targets should at least get _some_ roll (even a heavily penalized one) rather than a flat compare.
- **Did the Bog-Wretch ambush land while PCs were still Drowned, or miss the timing?** Landed hard — arguably harder than intended, since it hit while the party was mid-_rescue_ of an Incapacitated ally rather than merely Drowned. Surfaced a real gap: **this specific three-person roster has no Medicine skill and no healing Miracle at all.** The only recourse to Wren's Bleed-Out checks was two untrained Corvin Medicine rolls (2d6+0) — one failed (Death Mark), one succeeded (Triage/Stabilize) on the second attempt. That's a coin-flip on a party member's life with literally no skill investment behind it, which is a party-composition problem more than a system one, but worth flagging: a "playtest party" with zero Medicine/healing is a fragile default to test trauma-heavy scenes with.

## The Frost-Cave Troll

- **Did a Brace user take a hit? Outcome?** Yes — Helga (Block/shield-oriented). Took several hits across the fight; her shield's SV routinely reduced Impact to 0 or near it, but **Overwhelming Force's automatic 1 Stress on any Block vs. a Large enemy (win or lose) bled her out anyway** — she reached Breaking (100% Stress) despite never taking a Wound from a "real" hit, then took her first Wound purely from **Stress overflow** once maxed.
- **Did a Dodge/Parry user take a hit? Outcome?** Yes — Corvin (Parry-oriented). Took two real Wounds across the fight (one from a clean Impact ≥ Threshold, one from Stress overflow after a Snake Eyes on defense), and also generated the single most interesting offense of the fight via repeated Riposte triggers.
- **Conclusion: universal Elite-durability issue, or Brace-specific?** **Universal, not Brace-specific.** Both defense styles got tagged by the Troll's attacks, and both hit their own Breaking point independently. The actual bottleneck wasn't either PC's defense choice — it was **Troll-Blood Regeneration**, which healed 1 full Wound Slot at the start of every one of its activations. Across 9 simulated rounds, the party landed real Wounds on the Troll _five separate times_ (several via Corvin's Riposte, one via a clean Strike) and every single one was healed back before it could compound, because no single hit ever cleared 1 Wound Slot's worth of _net_ progress in a round where the Troll hadn't already acted. The only tool that reliably worked around it — Corvin's Momentum-fueled Surge for a second attack in the same round — needed a second **Margin-7+** win in the same round to actually stack two Wounds ahead of the heal, and that only happened by not happening: even with Surge active twice, only one of those attempts crossed the line. **No Fire source existed anywhere in this party's kit**, so the Weakness clause (which fully suppresses Regeneration) was never tested at all — this run cannot tell you whether Fire actually fixes the problem, only that _without_ it, the fight can stalemate indefinitely. The scene ended in a **full party retreat**, not a kill — both melee PCs Broken (maxed Stress) and carrying real Wounds, the Troll effectively undamaged. Recommend deciding whether "retreat as a valid outcome for a Major Scene" is acceptable design intent, or whether Regeneration needs a cap, a cooldown, or a mandatory in-fiction Fire source seeded somewhere in this specific scene if no partygenerates one itself.
- **Any Natural 12 + Margin 12+ double-dip observed?** No natural 12s came up across the entire session (Grave-Warden, Watch, Sluice Vault, or Troll fights) — the double-dip flag was never triggered, purely due to dice luck. Re-run recommended before ruling this out.

## General — other seams that surfaced

1. **Wound-slot-capacity ambiguity.** RAW ("taking a 4th Wound" for a 3-slot character) only defines Incapacitation as _exceeding_ capacity, never _exactly filling_ it. This came up three times: the Grave-Warden took a Major Wound that filled both its slots exactly (ruled: not yet dead, needs one more hit — and then died to that hit next round); a 1-Wound-Slot Bog-Wretch took its one Wound (ruled: dies outright, since a Fodder's whole design point is a 1-hit kill and the general rule clearly wasn't written with 1-slot creatures in mind); Corvin and Helga both later took Wounds purely from **Stress overflow** at their own cap, which the text does support explicitly. Recommend an explicit ruling for the multi-slot "exact fill" case and a carve-out for 1-slot Fodder.
2. **Snake Eyes has no defined behavior in an opposed Clash.** The consequence list ("Gear Degradation / Panic Reflex / Momentum Drain / Catastrophic Exposure") is explicitly scoped to unopposed checks. It came up three times this session in opposed rolls (once on a Resolve/Fear check, twice in combat Clashes) and each time we improvised a Panic Reflex-style extra Dissonant Stress on top of the automatic loss. Also had to rule whether Finesse's natural-1 reroll applies to a Snake Eyes double-1 — ruled no, since Snake Eyes' "regardless of...Gear modifiers" phrasing reads as an explicit override.
3. **Overwhelming Force only mentions Block, not Brace or Dodge**, against a Larger opponent. Whether that's deliberate (shields catch more surface area) or an oversight is worth a one-line confirmation — Helga switched from Block to Brace mid-fight specifically to dodge this tax, which worked, but felt like exploiting a gap rather than a real tactical choice.
4. **Instant Incapacitation (Impact ≥ 3× Threshold) doesn't say whether it also fills Wound Slots.** Ruled it does not — it's a pure status effect bypass. Result: Wren went unconscious with a perfectly clean 0/3 Wound Slot track, which reads strangely on a character sheet.
5. **A tie on Brace** (neither an explicit win nor loss per Brace's own binary phrasing) was ruled as a no-effect bind, consistent with the general Clash tie rule — but Brace's text doesn't actually cover this case.

## Bottom line

The Grave-Warden and Fear tests were compromised by this specific roster (no offensive Faith caster) and by tight-room geometry (no cover to Regroup away from Fear). The Watch pressure track worked exactly as designed — real tension, no forced fight. The Sluice Vault's Hazard Roll produced a legitimate, serious finding about Unaware targets having zero agency. The Frost-Cave Troll fight is the headline result: **Troll-Blood Regeneration, not Brace vs. Dodge/Parry, is the actual Elite-durability bottleneck**, and this party had no way to test the intended Fire-based fix. Recommend a follow-up run with (a) a Faith attacker for the Grave-Warden test, (b) a Medicine-trained party member, and (c) at least one Fire source in someone's kit before drawing final conclusions on the Troll.
