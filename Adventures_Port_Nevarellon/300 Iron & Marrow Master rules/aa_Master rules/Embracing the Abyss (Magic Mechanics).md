To keep the two systems distinct, we should root them in entirely opposite philosophies: Arcana is about Volatility and Margins, while Faith is about Certainty and Sacrifice.

Here is how we integrate them into the new engine.

### 1. Arcana (The Volatile Margin)

Arcana is the act of forcefully rewriting reality. It is highly illegal, mathematically devastating, and inherently unstable. It relies entirely on the new Margin mechanics.

The Arcane Clash (Combat Spells)

When an Arcanist casts an offensive spell (like Scorching Lance or Mind Fracture), they enter a Clash using Wits + Arcana.

- Spell Power: Spells have a "Power" rating exactly like weapons, which is added directly to the Arcanist's Strike roll.
    

The Margin of Manifestation (Utility Spells)

When casting an unopposed spell (like Levitate or Shatter Lock), the Arcanist rolls against the universal TN 8. The spell's effectiveness is entirely dictated by the Margin.

- Failure (<8): The spell fails. The Arcanist takes 1 Dissonant Stress.
    
- Messy Success (Margin 0–2): The spell works, but with dangerous collateral. The lock shatters, but the noise alerts the dungeon. The fire lights, but it catches the caster's sleeve. The caster takes 1 dissonant stress.
    
- Clean Success (Margin 3–4): The spell works exactly as intended.
    
- Exceptional (Margin 5+): The spell overcharges, generating 1 Momentum.
    

The "Snake Eyes" Backfire (Natural 2)

(DEV note) They immediately take 1 physical Wound (as their flesh chemically burns) AND 1 Dissonant Stress, and the spell produces a lethal hazard on the battlefield.(/DEV Note)

### 2. Faith (The Somatic Sacrifice)

Faith is not about channeling chaotic energy; it is about borrowing divine or eldritch authority. It is highly reliable but physically destroys the caster from the inside out.

The Locked Stress Economy

Faith magic almost never requires a 2d6 roll to see if it works. Instead, the Priest simply declares the Miracle, and it happens. The cost is paid directly in Locked Stress.

- The Mechanic: Casting a Miracle immediately fills 1 or more boxes on the Priest's Stress track with Locked Stress. This Stress cannot be cleared during a Breather or by spending Momentum. It permanently shrinks the Priest's mental threshold until they return to town and perform a religious Downtime Endeavour.
    
- The Attrition: A Priest can perfectly heal the party and strip the armor off bosses, but every time they do, they step closer to their own breaking point. When a Priest maxes out their Stress track, they cannot cast anymore without suffering physical Wounds (stigmata, bleeding from the eyes, shattered bones).
    

Miracles as Tag Manipulators

Because Faith bypasses the dice, its combat applications shouldn't just deal raw damage. Instead, Miracles rewrite the rules of the battlefield by manipulating Tags.

- Smite the Unclean (Cost: 1 Locked Stress): Automatically strips a target's Ablative Armor or Construct tag for the remainder of the encounter, allowing the Fighter's sword to finally deal full Impact.
    
- Aura of Repulsion (Cost: 2 Locked Stress): Creates a physical barrier. Fodder-tier enemies literally cannot enter the Priest's Threat Zone.
    
- Divine Triage (Cost: 1 Locked Stress): Used during a Breather. Automatically clears 1 Wound Slot from an ally without needing a Medicine check or alchemical supplies.
    

### 3. The Arcanist's Gamble (Pushing the Math)

To give Arcanists a tactical choice similar to the Priest's sacrifice, we can introduce Blood Channeling.

- Before rolling an Arcane check, the Arcanist can voluntarily take 1 Dissonant Stress (cutting their palm, inhaling toxic fumes) to automatically add a +2 ( or advantage?)modifier to their roll.
    
- This allows them to push the math to guarantee hitting TN 8 or to ensure a massive margin in a Clash, but it pushes them incredibly close to the Death Spiral.
    

### The Tabletop Dynamic

This creates two completely different playstyles for magic users:

- The Arcanist is an artillery cannon. They roll massive fistfuls of dice, chase high Margins for spectacular effects, and constantly flirt with catastrophic, Natural 2 explosions.
    
- The Priest is a resource manager. They are the anchor of the party, guaranteeing survival and breaking enemy defenses, but the player is constantly watching their Stress Limit shrink, knowing that every miracle brings them closer to a total psychological collapse.

To build those lists effectively under our new mechanics, it helps to step back and look at the big picture. When you design a tabletop RPG, you are essentially engaging in System Design.

In game development, system design is the process of creating the underlying rules, mathematical frameworks, and resource loops that dictate how a game behaves. It is the invisible scaffolding that turns a narrative concept into an interactive experience.

The fundamentals of system design can be broken down into five core principles. Here is how they apply to game design, and how they relate directly to what we are doing with Iron & Marrow:

### 1. The Core Resolution Mechanic (The Engine)

This is the foundational interaction pattern of your game. It is the primary way players translate an intention into a mechanical result.

- The Principle: It must be clean, repeatable, and intuitive. Every time a player wants to cross a boundary of uncertainty, they use this engine.
    
- In Iron & Marrow: Your engine is the 2d6 roll. We spent time adjusting this because your modifiers were overpowering the engine's built-in mathematical curve.
    

### 2. The Gameplay Loops (The Heartbeat)

As we discussed earlier, games are driven by cycles of action, feedback, and consequence. System design relies on mapping out these loops to ensure they feed into each other properly.

- The Principle: The output of a short-term loop must become the input for a long-term loop. If a loop dead-ends, the game stagnates.
    
- In Iron & Marrow: Winning a combat Clash generates Momentum (Micro-loop). You spend that Momentum during a Breather to clear Stress (Mid-loop). Clearing Stress keeps you healthy enough to finish the dungeon and return to town for Downtime Endeavours (Macro-loop). It’s a continuous chain.
    

### 3. Asymmetric Cognitive Load (The UI)

In board games, all players share the administrative rules. In tabletop RPGs, system designers must balance how much math the players handle versus how much the Game Master handles.

- The Principle: Players have the bandwidth for granular tracking because they only control one character. The GM controls the entire world, so their rules must be streamlined and binary to prevent burnout.
    
- In Iron & Marrow: We achieved this by keeping player Stress highly granular (-1 per point) but turning NPC Stress into a binary health bar (Fully Functional or Broken), and replacing NPC momentum tracking with a simple, fire-and-forget Threat Pool.
    

### 4. Mathematical Incentives (The "Meta")

Players do not always play games the way designers intend; they play games the way the math encourages them to play. If your lore says a game is about "desperate survival," but the math makes it incredibly easy to slaughter enemies without taking damage, your system is lying to the players.

- The Principle: You must design mechanics that mechanically enforce your game's intended tone.
    
- In Iron & Marrow: By making the Two-Way Clash symmetric (meaning a failed attack results in the enemy hitting you), the system mathematically incentivizes caution, tactical positioning, and teamwork, perfectly matching your gritty, transgressive setting.
    

### 5. Meaningful Space for Choice (The Strategy)

A good system design avoids "dominant strategies." A dominant strategy is a single option that is mathematically superior to all other choices in every situation (e.g., if "Attack" is always better than "Defend," defending is a dead mechanic).

- The Principle: Choices should have distinct trade-offs. One option shouldn't be better than another; it should be better for a specific context.
    
- In Iron & Marrow: We rebuilt Block, Parry, and Dodge around this principle. Block is mathematically safe but requires a shield. Parry is great with a heavy weapon but suicide with a dagger. Dodge strips the attacker's weapon power but risks total destruction if you fail.
    

### Moving Forward with Spell Lists

When we apply these system design principles to your traditional spell lists, our goal is to ensure that a spell isn't just a list of numbers—it needs to interact with these five fundamentals.

For example, a traditional Arcane spell shouldn't just say "Deals 4 damage." It should say something like:

Rusted Spike (Arcane): Roll a standard Wits + Arcana Clash against the target's Defense. If you win, deal Impact equal to your Margin. If you win with a Margin of 3+, the target's armor gains the Damaged tag.

This keeps the traditional feel of a spellbook while wiring the magic directly into your newly balanced 2d6 combat engine.

Environmental and utility spells—often called Control or Battlefield Manipulation magic—are some of the most satisfying traditional spells to design because they interact directly with your engine's environmental modifiers, tags, and action economy.

Instead of dealing raw damage or mitigating hits, these spells alter the physical conditions of the clash, resetting the mathematical baselines for everyone standing in the affected area.

Because these are traditional spells, they will have specific names and concrete rules, but they will be rolled using your standard unopposed TN 8 engine, with their duration, radius, or severity scaling beautifully with the Margin of Success.

Here are four traditional environmental spells designed to integrate seamlessly into the Iron & Marrow chassis.

### 1. Choking Vapor (Arcane)

The caster exhales a thick, localized cloud of roiling, yellow-black sulfurous fog.

- Resolution: Unopposed Wits + Arcana vs. TN 8.
    
- The Effect: Creates a 15-foot zone of dense fog centered on a point within sight. Any ranged attack passing through or targeting someone inside the fog suffers a -2 penalty.
    
- The Margin Scaler:
    

- Margin 0–2 (Messy): The fog manifests, but a stray gust blows it slightly. The GM shifts the center of the fog 5 feet in a random direction.
    
- Margin 3–4 (Clean): The fog settles perfectly.
    
- Margin 5+ (Exceptional): The vapor is incredibly toxic. Any creature that ends its turn inside the fog must pass a TN 8 Resolve check or immediately suffer 1 Dissonant Stress.
    

### 2. Mire (Arcane)

The caster slams their staff into the earth, liquefying stone, soil, or wood into a clutching, viscous mud.

- Resolution: Unopposed Wits + Arcana vs. TN 8.
    
- The Effect: A 10x10 foot area of ground becomes difficult terrain. Any creature entering or starting their turn in the mire has their movement speed halved. Furthermore, characters cannot use the Dodge action while standing in the mire—the mud makes nimble footwork mathematically impossible. They must rely on Block or Parry.
    
- The Margin Scaler:
    

- Margin 0–2 (Messy): The mud forms, but the back-surge forces the caster to immediately take 1 Dissonant Stress from the physical strain.
    
- Margin 3–4 (Clean): The mire forms flawlessly.
    
- Margin 5+ (Exceptional): The mud is incredibly deep. One Elite or Fodder creature currently standing in the area is instantly Anchored and must waste their next Aggressor action pulling themselves free.
    

### 3. Brittle-Iron Aura (Arcane)

The caster hums a discordant, high-pitched note that vibrates violently through the air, causing metal structures and gear to crystallize and weaken.

- Resolution: Unopposed Wits + Arcana vs. TN 8.
    
- The Effect: The caster chooses an environmental object made of metal (a iron portcullis, a heavy chain, a barred door). The object's structural durability is compromised. Any subsequent physical attempt to shatter, break, or force it open gains Advantage on the roll.
    
- The Margin Scaler:
    

- Margin 3+ (Clean): The structural degradation is severe enough that it can be broken with ordinary tools, bypassing the need for heavy siege gear.
    
- Margin 5+ (Exceptional): The frequency is so precise it bleeds into active combat gear. The caster can choose one Elite enemy in the room; that enemy's armor or shield immediately gains the Damaged tag, reducing its effectiveness for the rest of the fight.
    

### 4. Ward of the Threshold (Faith / Miracle)

The Priest traces a sacred rune in the air over a doorway, archway, or narrow pass, forbidding entry to the corrupted.

- Resolution: No roll required. Paid directly in Locked Stress.
    
- Cost: 1 Locked Stress.
    
- The Effect: A glowing, invisible barrier seals a narrow choke point (up to 10 feet wide). Enemies possessing the Undead or Void-Touched tags physically cannot cross the threshold.
    
- The Attrition Loop: The ward holds for the duration of the encounter, or until a Dread/Boss entity spends 1 Threat point to violently shatter the barrier. If a Boss shatters the ward, the feedback instantly inflicts 1 Dissonant Stress on the Priest.
    

### Why These Spells Perfect the Gameplay Loop

1. They Dictate the Meta: If a party is facing a pack of Feral mutant hounds that rely on Pack Tactics and movement to flank, a smart Arcanist drops Mire. By cutting the hounds' speed and disabling their Dodge, the Arcanist has fundamentally altered the math of the fight without ever rolling a single damage die.
    
2. They Interact with Monster Tags: These spells create a direct dialogue with your Bestiary. A Construct might be immune to the Choking Vapor's stress effect because it doesn't breathe, but it might be completely devastated by Mire because its Unstoppable Mass makes it too heavy to maneuver out of the mud.
    
3. They Maintain the Core Tension: Because the Arcane spells use the Margin Scaler, casting Mire or Choking Vapor in a tight hallway is still a gamble. A "Messy Success" means you got the cover you wanted, but now your own team is choking on the fumes or the Arcanist is losing their mental grip.
